# 2. Related work

Four strands organize prior work. Each converts a possible originality objection into cited lineage and names what remains ours.

## 2.1 Uncertified readout consumption in knowledge tracing

Deep KT models increasingly ship latent-state readouts presented as measurement. Deep-IRT attaches an IRT output layer to a memory network and presents the resulting ability and difficulty estimates as explainable by construction (Yeung 2019). PSI-KT claims interpretability by design for its learner traits and inferred prerequisite graph (Zhou et al. 2024). The graph line extracts inter-skill structure directly, from the influence graph of the original DKT (Piech et al. 2015) through GKT (Nakagawa et al. 2019) and GIKT (Yang et al. 2020). In every case the validation offered is predictive, an AUC gain or a face-valid inspection, never a test of whether the estimator recovers known structure.

Two neighbors touch signed cross-skill structure and require exact placement. HawkesKT models temporal cross-effects between skills through mutual excitation (Wang et al. 2021). Its cross-effect parameters are signed, but they are uncertified and response-conditioned. The signs are interpreted for prerequisite discovery, the negative direction arises when a wrong answer on one skill depresses the prediction on another rather than as a skill-to-skill interference estimate, and the sole external check is ranking agreement with expert annotators on a collapsed positive-only score (NDCG 0.83) [S21]. LTKT models concurrent positive and negative learning transfer among concepts, and we credit it for that framing (Xu et al. 2025). Its signed graph, however, is a stipulated input rather than a model readout. The signs come from a hand-designed co-occurrence heuristic, frozen before training and computed over training, validation, and test data jointly, a leakage the paper itself states, and validation is predictive ablation only. Neither work asks whether its estimator recovers known signs, and neither reports a detection floor. Our association claim therefore rests on a three-way conjunction, signs recovered from the fitted model, certified against ground truth with designed confound arms, and accompanied by a harness-measured detectable-dose floor.

The 2025-26 newcomers continue the pattern. KeenKT, UKT, and PAKT enrich the readout itself, with distributional mastery states (Li et al. 2025), uncertainty-aware embeddings (Cheng et al. 2025), and an ability-proficiency decomposition (PAKT 2026), and KTCF generates counterfactual recourse from KT models (Kim, Lee, and Kim 2026); all validate by prediction benchmarks. The audit-flavored newcomers stop short of the latent state. They re-evaluate experimental protocols (Kim and Kim 2026), audit deployment behavior beyond AUC (Yamkovenko et al. 2025), measure robustness under concept drift (Lee et al. 2025), or ask whether interpretable KT changes teacher decisions (Khalid, Deriyeva, and Paassen 2025). The closest neighbor in spirit corrects per-item bias in frozen KT models through empirical-Bayes shrinkage (Yan, Tang, and Shimada 2026). That line repairs prediction logits on real data, without ground truth and without certification; we certify readouts before any claim is read and refuse those that fail. A verification sweep found both of this paper's target claims, signed influence recovery and a trustworthiness audit of KT readouts, genuinely open [S21].

## 2.2 Growth detection and its known limits

Detecting learning in log data is prior art. The P(J) detector estimates the probability that a student learned a skill at a specific step (Baker, Goldstein, and Heffernan 2011). Learning-factors analysis fits per-skill learning-rate slopes with standard inference (Cen, Koedinger, and Junker 2006), and iAFM extends slope inference at scale (Koedinger et al. 2023). None of this is certified against ground truth, and the learning curves it rests on can be artifacts of attrition (Nixon et al. 2018). The limits are equally well documented. Observed performance is compatible with families of parameter estimates that predict identically yet make different knowledge claims (Beck and Chang 2007; van de Sande 2013; Doroudi and Brunskill 2017), the predictive advantage of deep KT is largely explained without per-skill tracking (Khajah, Lindsey, and Mozer 2016), and ceiling effects and wheel-spinning bound what near-mastered data can reveal (Beck and Gong 2013). We cite this literature ahead of our results deliberately. When the protocol later refuses per-skill resolution or diagnoses a false fire under saturation, the content of those verdicts echoes these ancestors; what the protocol adds is a certified, dataset-specific verdict with the mechanism attached. Detection and slope inference are prior art. Certification is not.

## 2.3 Methodological lineage

The protocol's ingredients have owners, and we cite them before a reviewer does. Simulation-based calibration validates inference machinery on data simulated from the model before trusting real-data inferences (Talts et al. 2018). Randomization-based sanity checks showed that widely used interpretability readouts fail null tests they should pass (Adebayo et al. 2018). Permutation nulls and negative-control designs are standard practice in statistics and epidemiology (Lipsitch et al. 2010). Minimum detectable effects have two lineages we draw on, the psychometric power literature on detecting change (Hertzog et al. 2006; Rast and Hofer 2014) and the minimum-detectable-effect tradition imported into NLP benchmarking (Card et al. 2020). Parameter recovery on synthetic data is routine in KT itself (Lan et al. 2014), and a recent pre-fit diagnostic asks whether neural interaction discoveries are recoverable at all, the same epistemic move in a different domain (Kuskova, Zaytsev, and Coppedge 2026). What we claim is not the epistemic move. It is the assembly for KT latent-state readouts, and one device within it, the matched synthetic twin, a replica calibrated to a named real dataset with the readout's target switched on and off, bracketing the density range with profiles at both extremes [M2]. None of the cited lines constructs that object or gates real-data claims on its verdicts.

## 2.4 Change measurement and the validity argument

Measuring change is an old psychometric problem, with a formal tradition of structured models for growth and a long-running debate over the reliability of difference scores; the power to detect change, and individual differences in change, has been quantified in that tradition for decades (Hertzog et al. 2006). We concede the test forms here are classical. The permutation gate, the corridor bars, and the floor measurements assemble standard machinery. What we take from validity theory is the frame. Kane's interpretation and use arguments treat validation as an explicit chain of inferences, each of which must be licensed before the interpretation it supports is used. The protocol operationalizes that program for the readouts of deep KT models. Every cell of the licensing map is such an inference, licensed with conditions or refused with a mechanism, and a refusal is an explicitly unlicensed inference published as a result rather than buried, in the register of Mayo's severe testing. This is not offered as a contribution to validity theory. The contribution is the working protocol and the map it produces for the readouts a fielded KT model publishes.
