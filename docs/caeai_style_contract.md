## 1. Heading grammar

**Dominant pattern.**
- **Numbered decimal, IMRaD-anchored.** Top-level sections carry Arabic
  numbers (`1.`, `2.`), subsections one dot (`3.1.`), sub-subsections two
  (`3.1.1.`). Depth rarely exceeds three. The spine is Introduction to
  Related work (or Background / Literature review) to Method (or Materials
  and methods) to Results to Discussion to Conclusion, with the back matter
  unnumbered.
- **Sentence case, not title case.** Capitalize the first word and proper
  nouns only ("Data analysis", not "Data Analysis").
- **Noun phrases, not sentences.** Section headings name an object of study
  or a procedure. Declarative-sentence headings and rhetorical/essay headings
  are off-pattern for the body.
- **Colon policy.** Colons are common in the *article title* ("Main claim:
  scope qualifier") and acceptable in *subsection* headings, but rare in
  top-level section headings. Prefer a colon-free noun phrase for `\section`.
- **Back matter (Elsevier fixed wording).** "Declaration of competing
  interest", "CRediT authorship contribution statement", "Data availability",
  "Funding", "Acknowledgements" appear verbatim and unnumbered.

**Minority variants.** Some reviews/position pieces drop numbering or use
"Theoretical background". A few empirical papers use lightly structured or
colon-bearing section headings. The numbered-sentence-case-noun-phrase form is
the safe majority.

## 2. Caption anatomy

**Figure captions.**
- **Label.** "Fig. N." (abbreviated, period after the number). Placed *below*
  the figure.
- **First unit.** A short declarative summary or noun phrase naming what the
  figure shows. A one-clause interpretive takeaway is allowed and common
  (CAEAI captions may state the finding, not only label the axes).
- **Panel spec.** Multi-panel figures use "(a) ... (b) ..." with the panel
  letter leading each panel's clause. "Left/right" is a tolerated minority
  form; "(a)/(b)" is preferred and assumes the panels are lettered in the
  artwork.
- **Stats/notes placement.** Sample size, error-bar definition, and CI
  meaning ride at the *end* of the caption ("Error bars are 95% confidence
  intervals"; "n = ...").
- **Length band.** Roughly 1 to 4 sentences, about 15 to 60 words. Axis-only
  captions and half-page captions both read as off-pattern.

*Representative-to-pattern figure caption:*
> **Fig. 4.** Recovery of item discrimination across training budgets. (a)
> Shared readout; (b) decoupled readout. Points are means over 25 fits; error
> bars are seed-clustered 95% confidence intervals.

**Table captions and notes.**
- **Label.** "Table N" placed *above* the table (no trailing period on the
  number in Elsevier style; a period or the caption text follows).
- **First unit.** A noun-phrase title of the table's contents.
- **Notes.** A "Note." block *below* the table carries sample size, units,
  emphasis conventions, and significance keys. Descriptive material may also
  be folded into the caption sentence, which CAEAI tolerates; significance
  stars belong in the note.
- **Significance key (APA family).** `*p < .05, **p < .01, ***p < .001`.

*Representative-to-pattern table caption + note:*
> **Table 2.** Descriptive statistics and pairwise model comparison on the
> held-out set.
> *Note.* N = 3,000. Best value in each column in bold. Brackets are 95%
> confidence intervals. \*p < .05, \*\*p < .01, \*\*\*p < .001.

## 3. Abstract and keywords norms

- **Abstract.** Unstructured, single paragraph, roughly 150 to 250 words. No
  citations, no undefined abbreviations, no display math. State problem,
  approach, key quantitative result, and contribution. (Minority variant:
  a few empirical CAEAI papers use light Purpose/Method/Results/Conclusion
  labels; unstructured single paragraph is the majority.)
- **Keywords.** 1 to 7, English, sentence case, most commonly 4 to 6. Ordered
  general to specific. Rendered comma- or semicolon-separated depending on the
  class.
- **Register.** Scholarly and hedged-precise. Prefer "substantially
  inaccurate" over "badly wrong" in the abstract; keep colloquial framing out.

## 4. Voice and statistics reporting norms

- **Voice.** First-person plural "we" for authorial acts is accepted in this
  ML-flavored venue and is the majority in method papers. Past tense for what
  was done and found; present tense for standing facts and what a figure
  shows. Avoid rhetorical questions and essayistic asides in the body.
- **Numbers as words vs numerals.** Numerals for measured quantities, counts
  with units, and anything a reader will compare (25 fits, 60 items, top-20
  flags). Spell out only a number that opens a sentence.
- **Statistics (APA author-date family).**
  - Report the estimate, its dispersion or interval, and the test where one
    exists. Label the first confidence interval "95% CI [lo, hi]"; bracket-only
    intervals thereafter are fine if defined once.
  - Effect sizes accompany significance tests where applicable.
  - **Leading zeros.** APA-strict drops the leading zero on quantities bounded
    by 1 (correlations, *p*): `r = .99`, `p < .001`. The manuscript instead
    keeps leading zeros throughout (`r = 0.99`, recoveries `0.719`), which is
    common and defensible in ML-flavored CAEAI papers. **Pick one convention
    and hold it paper-wide.** Given the manuscript's density of bounded
    metrics reported as generic scores, retaining leading zeros for internal
    consistency is the lower-risk choice; the proposals in Section 5 keep them
    to match the body.
  - **Uncertainty language.** Keep the exact "seed-clustered bootstrap 95%
    confidence interval" description; spell out "confidence interval" on first
    use, then "CI".
- **References.** Author-date (APA family), consistent with the sister
  journal. The manuscript's `natbib` `authoryear` + `apalike` is compatible;
  at submission switch to `elsarticle` with an APA-style bibliography.

> At N = 500 the same ordering holds with a larger ability-noise term (0.14

> **Table 4.** Simulated adaptive-testing cost, LSTM encoder with the
