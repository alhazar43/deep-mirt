% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{The setup, and the question}
Neural knowledge tracing exposes IRT-style parameters, a per-step ability
$\theta$, item discrimination $\alpha$, step thresholds $\beta$, but it is trained
to \textbf{predict the next response}, not to estimate those parameters.
\medskip

The IRT structure is the \emph{flavor}, the route to $\hat y$. The objective is a
prediction loss on $y$ vs $\hat y$ (cross-entropy / weighted ordinal loss on the
decoder logits), not a model-wise likelihood.
\medskip

\begin{block}{Question}
Under a prediction objective, \textbf{which} IRT parameters does the model recover,
how \textbf{fast}, and \textbf{why}?
\end{block}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{The spine: prediction pins a parameter by its Fisher leverage}
A prediction loss constrains a parameter only through that parameter's
\textbf{leverage on $\hat y$}, how much the predicted response distribution moves
when the parameter moves. That leverage \emph{is} the Fisher information.
\medskip

For the 2PL/GPCM,
\[
  I(\theta) = a^2\,\mathrm{Var}_k(k), \qquad
  I(\alpha) \propto (\theta-\beta)^2\,P(1-P)\ \ \to 0 \ \text{at}\ \theta=\beta .
\]
Discrimination is the \textbf{low-information} parameter (classical result; its
information vanishes exactly where targeted responses concentrate).
\medskip

\begin{alertblock}{Prediction (testable)}
$\theta,\beta$ are high-leverage, prediction pins them. $\alpha$ is low-leverage,
prediction recovers it poorly, and a richer readout should help \emph{it}
specifically.
\end{alertblock}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Act 1, the benefit of a dynamic $\alpha$ tracks Fisher stiffness}
\begin{columns}
\begin{column}{0.52\textwidth}
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fisher_ratio.png}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.46\textwidth}
\small
State-conditioning the \textbf{low-Fisher} $\alpha$ (occurrence-averaged) helps its
recovery; doing the same to the high-Fisher $\beta$ does nothing
($\overline{\Delta\beta}\approx0$). Parameter-specific, not generic flexibility.
\medskip

The benefit $\Delta\alpha$ grows with the GPCM stiffness $I(\theta)/I(\alpha)$
(Pearson on $\log$-stiffness $+0.87$). At $K{=}2$ stiffness $\approx 1$ and the
dynamic head only adds noise ($\Delta\alpha<0$); the benefit switches on as
stiffness climbs.
\medskip

\textbf{Counter to static theory:} classical estimation finds $K$ \emph{negligible}
(or harmful) for static $\alpha$ recovery (per-category dilution). The dynamic,
occurrence-pooled readout escapes that dilution.
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Act 2, the advantage is a convergence-rate effect (grounded)}
\begin{columns}
\begin{column}{0.62\textwidth}
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_convergence.png}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.36\textwidth}
\small
Not an earlier start, a \textbf{mid-training acceleration}, then a permanent
\textbf{endpoint gap that grows with $K$}: static $\alpha$ is trapped by the
stiffness ceiling, the dynamic head breaks through.
\medskip

This is the IRT instance of a known principle, per-direction learning speed set by
information-geometry conditioning (Saxe 2014; Cao 2019, NTK; Transtrum 2015,
sloppy models). \textbf{Grounded, not a black box.}
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Act 3, dynamic $\alpha$ detects ability-dependent discrimination}
\begin{columns}
\begin{column}{0.62\textwidth}
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_rq3.png}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.36\textwidth}
\small
Plant $\alpha_{\text{eff}}=a\,e^{\gamma\theta}$ (matched null). Read as the linear
slope of $\log\alpha$ on the \emph{true} $\theta$, the model \textbf{detects} the
planted dependence in \textbf{rank} (corr $0.4$ to $0.67$, dose-responsive,
null$\approx 0$).
\medskip

But \textbf{magnitude is not recovered} ($k\!\approx\!0.04$, a $\sim$30$\times$
shrinkage; ruled clean of the $\theta$-scale confound, $k/c{=}0.034$ at $c{=}1.14$).
Prediction cannot pin $\alpha$'s magnitude where its leverage vanishes.
\medskip

\textbf{Caution:} the \emph{size} of a dynamic $\alpha$ is not the strength of
context-dependence.
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Endpoint recovery, and reproducibility}
\begin{columns}
\begin{column}{0.55\textwidth}
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_endpoint.png}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.43\textwidth}
\small
On the dimension prior work competes on (endpoint recovery), the model is strong:
at $K{=}4$, $\theta/\alpha/\beta$ recover at $0.93/0.94/0.99$ (Pearson $r$). At
$K{=}2$ the \emph{static} config is indicated, the dynamic readout is for high
stiffness (Act 1).
\medskip

Raw RMSE is not meaningful for a prediction-trained model (latent scale
unidentified), so we report scale-aligned RMSE alongside Pearson, the same
story: prediction fixes \emph{shape}, not absolute scale.
\medskip

The nearest competitor (DKVMN robust-2PL estimation, Tsutsumi lineage) reports
endpoint RMSE only, with \textbf{no code or checkpoints}. We release both.
\end{column}

\begin{frame}{Where this sits}
\textbf{Prior art (adversarial sweep).} The learning-\emph{dynamics} of IRT-parameter
recovery by a prediction-trained model is \textbf{open}, every neighbor (Deep-IRT,
VIBO, CEN, the DKVMN robust-2PL thesis) reports \emph{endpoint} recovery only.
\medskip

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(b) convergence dynamics} \,--\, \emph{grounded} in DL learning-dynamics
theory. The validation result, the model behaves as theory predicts.
\item \textbf{(a) $K$-growing, parameter-specific $\alpha$ benefit} \,--\, \emph{novel},
and counter to static estimation theory. The hook.
\item \textbf{(c) rank-yes / magnitude-no detection} \,--\, \emph{novel}. Neural KT
state-conditions \emph{difficulty} (SAD-IRT, NCDM), not discrimination; the
classical relatives are person-discrimination (D2PMM, $\theta$-independent) and
non-uniform DIF (group-level, not within-person). Detecting ability-dependent
discrimination in rank vs magnitude has no prior treatment.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Takeaway}
\begin{block}{One line}
A prediction-trained neural IRT recovers each parameter \textbf{in proportion to its
Fisher leverage on the prediction}.
\end{block}
\medskip
\begin{itemize}
\item Making the low-leverage discrimination \textbf{dynamic} buys a $K$-growing
convergence-rate advantage, opposite to what static estimation predicts.
\item It recovers genuine ability-dependent discrimination in \textbf{rank, not
magnitude}, because prediction cannot pin what it has no leverage on.
\item The dynamics are \textbf{grounded} in known learning-dynamics theory, an
interpretable, not black-box, neural IRT.
\end{itemize}
\medskip
\footnotesize Caveats, synthetic GPCM, preliminary seeds, single backbone. Next,
real cross-subject data ($\alpha_{\text{static}}$ invariance, within-student
$\alpha$-tracking).
\end{frame}
