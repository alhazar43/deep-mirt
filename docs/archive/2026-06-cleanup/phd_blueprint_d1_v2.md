# D1, Round 4. Closed-Loop Educational AI on a Streaming Measurement Primitive

A blueprint for Wenrui Yuan's thesis. This is a piece of thinking, not a literature compilation. Length sits between three and five thousand words. Citations are limited to work that is genuinely load-bearing.

## 1. Why closed-loop is the right thesis claim, and why now

The boring version of educational measurement runs in batches. You collect a response matrix, run EM, get item parameters, freeze them, then deploy a policy that uses those parameters. Calibration and policy are structurally decoupled. The decoupling is not a design choice, it is forced by the math. EM needs the matrix complete enough to identify item parameters, and the policy needs item parameters to act. So calibration happens first, action happens second, and in between you wait.

That waiting is gone. Modern learning platforms produce response streams, not response matrices. Items enter and leave the bank during deployment, generated by LLMs or by content teams. Cohorts are open, students arrive mid-course. The apparatus itself is non-stationary because LLM graders shift, generative items have noisy psychometric properties at birth, and instructional content reorganizes around what works. None of this can be served by the batch decoupling. You cannot calibrate first when there is no first.

This is the actual condition that motivates the thesis. It is not that streaming is faster than batch. It is that the batch primitive is no longer well-defined. The thesis claim is that measurement and action have to be one computational object, because in streaming they share the same observations and influence the same latents. Every action is a measurement (it produces a response from which we infer ability) and every measurement is an action (it changes what the student sees next, and thereby their trajectory). Treating them as separate stages is an artifact of when the response matrix was the unit of analysis.

Closed-loop is the right framing because it is the only one that makes the streaming primitive deliver value beyond efficiency. A pure streaming measurement primitive (D2) gives you faster theta, but you can still hand off to a downstream policy with stale parameters and lose half the value. A pure recommendation thread (DRL on tutoring) gives you a policy but the observation model is hand-wavy, and the field has been burned for a decade by policies whose value depends on unmodeled drift in the underlying psychometrics. Closing the loop is what forces the two halves to be honest about each other.

Now is the right time for three reasons. First, the deep psychometrics work of the last five years (DKT, AKT, deep-IRT, DKVMN-based recovery, and the candidate's own MA-GPCM) has converged on the empirical fact that neural sequence models can match EM on parameter recovery while running in streaming form. So the measurement primitive exists. Second, the off-policy and bandit literature has gotten serious about partially identified reward and observation models, which is exactly the condition you face when the observation model is a learned theta estimator [reward learning is partially identifiable, see Skalse et al. 2022 on invariance in reward learning]. Third, LLM-generated items have arrived in production with essentially no psychometric infrastructure for them, which means there is a research vacuum precisely where streaming calibration meets streaming policy.

What would a strict reviewer attack? Four lines, in order of severity.

(a) "Closed-loop is just bandit-meets-IRT, you are repackaging." The defense is that bandit-IRT (BanditCAT, AutoIRT and their predecessors) treats the IRT parameters as a fixed reward model and runs the bandit on top. That is two-stage, not closed-loop. The thesis claim is that the measurement primitive itself updates inside the policy loop and the policy is evaluated against the measurement primitive that produced it. Different object.

(b) "Identifiability collapses when the policy depends on theta and theta depends on the policy's selected items." This is the strongest attack and one I take seriously below. It is genuinely a new identifiability problem and the thesis must address it head on.

(c) "You cannot run RCTs in real classrooms to validate closed-loop, so all of this is simulation theatre." Partly true, partly answerable. The defense is that streaming calibration produces a per-student, per-time counterfactual prediction that can be validated against held-out responses, which is a weaker but real form of evaluation. Sim-to-real is a separate thrust, not a hand-wave.

(d) "AIED reviewers will not understand the streaming primitive framing, ML reviewers will not understand the measurement primitive." This is a venue strategy problem, not a research problem. Addressed in section 8.

## 2. Thrusts, derived from the reasoning

I am not going to pick three or five thrusts and then back-fill. Let me work through what the framing actually requires.

The thesis claim is "measurement and action are one streaming object in education." For that to be a thesis and not a slogan, you need (a) the measurement primitive, (b) the action primitive operating on top of it, (c) a proof that the coupling delivers something neither alone can, (d) the new identifiability and evaluation problems that the coupling creates, (e) the bank-and-apparatus side (items, drift, fairness) because measurement-action on a corrupt item bank is just amplified bias, and (f) a deployment story because the whole thing is meaningless if it cannot live on a real platform.

That gives five natural thrusts. They are not topics, they are *roles* in the argument.

**Thrust 1, Measurement primitive.** MA-GPCM and its immediate generalizations. Reason it exists. You cannot close a loop without a measurement object that updates in stream and remains identifiable. Open intellectual problem. Whether streaming theta and item parameter updates can be done jointly without each one corrupting the other, and whether multidimensional theta is recoverable from streaming alone (with no Q-matrix prior). Success means streaming joint calibration matches batch EM on recovery RMSE, and beats it on within-session ability tracking. Falsifier. If streaming theta is unbiased only when item parameters are frozen, then the measurement primitive collapses back to two-stage and the thesis loses its foundation.

**Thrust 2, Action primitive on streaming measurement.** Policy that ingests the live measurement object, not stale parameters. Reason it exists. The candidate has DRL background, but more importantly the closed-loop argument requires that the policy be defined as a function of the current measurement state and not a function of a snapshot. Open problem. Off-policy evaluation when the observation model (theta estimator) is itself learned and drifting. State representation when the latent is non-Markovian (student forgetting, fatigue) and you only have proxies. Success means a policy that on streaming benchmarks beats both (i) a fixed-parameter bandit and (ii) a batch-recalibrated bandit at fixed sample cost, with confidence intervals derived under partial identifiability of the reward. Falsifier. If the variance from coupling measurement and policy update dominates the gain from being current, closed-loop loses to two-stage with periodic recalibration.

**Thrust 3, Closed-loop validity.** This is the thrust nobody else will do for you. Reason it exists. Coupling creates two new problems that do not exist in either pure measurement or pure policy work. First, off-policy validity when the observation function (theta) is non-stationary because the policy is changing what is observed. Second, identifiability of latent dynamics under intervention, because the same theta trajectory could be explained by learning or by the policy's content choice. Open problem. What estimands are even identified in closed-loop educational measurement. How do we get honest counterfactuals about "what would theta have done under a different policy." Success means a formal characterization of identified versus partially identified estimands, and an estimator that achieves nominal coverage under the partial identification. Falsifier. If the identified set is so wide that no policy is comparable to any other, closed-loop is observationally vacuous and the thesis must retreat to D2.

**Thrust 4, Bank, apparatus, drift.** Reason it exists. Closed-loop on a corrupt bank is amplified bias. If items are generated by an LLM and enter the bank with noisy psychometric properties, the policy will exploit those noisy properties. So the bank side of the streaming primitive (item calibration, DIF, generative item vetting, fairness) has to live inside the thesis, not as a follow-up. Open problem. Streaming DIF detection that does not require a pre-specified group structure (because in open cohorts the group structure is itself emergent). Calibration of LLM-generated items from cold-start with bandit-driven exposure. Success means generative items can be inducted into the bank with calibrated uncertainty that the policy respects, and DIF/drift signals can fire in stream with controlled false discovery. Falsifier. If LLM items cannot be calibrated fast enough to be safe in policy use, the closed-loop scope shrinks to fixed banks, which is the easy case and not the thesis.

**Thrust 5, Sim-to-real and deployment.** Reason it exists. Educational AI has a credibility problem with simulation-only results. If the thesis only ever runs on synthetic DGPs and ASSISTments, the closed-loop framing remains philosophical. Open problem. How to validate a closed-loop primitive when the only available real data is observational. Where simulation has to be faithful (drift, cohort opening, item bank turnover) and where it can be cartoon (exact response generation function). Success means at least one deployment, even small, on a real platform, with held-out streaming validation that supports the simulation-derived claims. Falsifier. If the simulation results do not transfer to the smallest deployment, the framing is rhetorical.

Five thrusts. I do not love that there are exactly five, but I cannot fold any pair without losing the argument.

## 3. Where MA-GPCM sits

MA-GPCM is Thrust 1's anchor paper and the existence proof for the rest. Its essential property is that it produces a per-time, per-student theta estimate alongside item parameters using one streaming forward pass, without an EM-style re-pass over the data, and that the parameters it recovers are competitive with batch EM on synthetic recovery. That is the property the rest of the thesis depends on. Everything else about MA-GPCM (the GPCM head, the DKVMN backbone, the separated-ability pathway, the SIE encoding) is incidental to the thesis framing. The model could be replaced by AKT-with-IRT-head or by some future transformer-based streaming psychometrician and the closed-loop claim would survive.

What makes MA-GPCM the right *first* paper under the framing is not that it is the best architecture, it is that it is the first object in this space that lets you say "I have a streaming measurement primitive that you can build a policy on top of without lying about its psychometric content." That is a different claim from "I have a knowledge tracing model that predicts the next response," which is what DKT-style work delivers, and a different claim from "I have item parameters" which is what neural-IRT delivers.

A separate question is whether MA-GPCM should be presented as the foundation or as the running example. My instinct is foundation. It is referenced in every subsequent thrust as the measurement object. The risk of running-example framing is that reviewers read the thesis as "MA-GPCM and four extensions," which is exactly the misreading the candidate has spent rounds 1 through 3 trying to avoid.

One honest concern. MA-GPCM in its current form is unidimensional, fixed-bank, single-cohort, no within-session generative items. So MA-GPCM as published is the floor. Thrust 1 needs at least one substantial extension paper that moves the measurement primitive into multidimensional theta, joint streaming calibration (item parameters update with theta), and open cohorts. Without that, Thrust 1 is one paper and the thrust does not exist as a thrust.

## 4. Where DRL fits

DRL is the action primitive. Not "the recommender thread." The framing matters because if DRL is a parallel thread, the thesis is two PhDs glued together, which is exactly the failure mode rounds 1 through 3 were running into.

What the candidate's DRL background buys is concrete. Familiarity with off-policy evaluation, with policy gradient and value-based methods, with the bandit-to-RL spectrum, with simulation-based validation, and with the practical question of how to ship a policy. Without that background the thrust would not be feasible inside one PhD.

What is genuinely new technical work, beyond standard DRL.

(i) Policy classes defined on a streaming psychometric state, not on a fixed feature vector. The state is the measurement primitive's internal representation (memory in DKVMN, or theta plus uncertainty in IRT-style). This is non-Markovian under realistic forgetting, and the policy has to handle that.

(ii) Reward design under partial identifiability of the underlying ability. You cannot use theta itself as the reward because theta is identified up to monotone transformations under classical IRT and is even less identified in the streaming case. So reward has to be defined on observables (next-response correctness, mastery threshold crossing, externally validated outcomes) and the policy has to respect that the reward is a noisy proxy for the latent.

(iii) Off-policy evaluation when the observation model is learned. Standard OPE assumes you know the propensity, and modern OPE assumes you can model the observation. Here the observation function (theta from sequence) is itself learned and drifting. This is the technical heart of Thrust 3 and is not solved in standard DRL.

(iv) Sample efficiency. Educational deployments have orders of magnitude fewer samples than RL benchmarks. You cannot run twenty million episodes. Policy classes need to be small or strongly regularized by the measurement primitive, and that is research, not engineering.

I want to be clear about one thing. The DRL part is the easier half if you treat it as standard contextual bandits. It is the harder half if you treat it as actually-closed-loop with a learned observation model. The thesis needs the harder framing for the closed-loop claim to be earned.

## 5. Closed-loop as a technical construction, precisely

Here is the construction. There is a streaming response stream of (student, item, response, time). The measurement primitive M is a parametric model that maintains posterior or point estimates over theta_s(t) for each student s and over item parameters (alpha_q, beta_q) for each item q, updated online. The action primitive A is a policy pi(a_t | s_t) that chooses the next item a_t for the active student given the streaming state s_t produced by M. Closed-loop means three things hold simultaneously.

First, the policy is defined as a function of M's current state. Not on a snapshot. Not on yesterday's calibration.

Second, M is updated using the responses elicited under pi, including the fact that pi chose those items. So the item selection is non-ignorable for theta estimation. Classical IRT can ignore selection if items are missing at random (the test designer chose them in advance). In closed-loop, items are missing not at random and depend on theta itself. M has to handle that or it is biased.

Third, evaluation of pi is done against M, and M is evaluated against held-out responses. This creates a circularity that has to be broken explicitly. The usual break is a two-time-scale argument. M updates fast, pi updates slow, and pi's value is evaluated against a frozen-at-time-t copy of M with cross-fitting on held-out subsequences. This is the construction the thesis has to make rigorous.

Concretely, the new technical problems.

(A) Selection bias in streaming theta. When pi selects items adaptively, the responses observed are a non-uniform sample of the response surface. Theta estimators that ignore this will be biased toward the easy-item regions where the policy lingers. The fix is to model the selection process inside M, either by inverse-propensity weighting in the likelihood, or by explicitly including the action in the generative model. Neither is standard practice in deep psychometrics today.

(B) Off-policy validity with a learned observation model. Standard OPE assumes a known or modeled reward. Here the reward is some function of theta, and theta is learned from data that depends on the policy that generated it. This is partially identified. The honest answer is to return a set-valued estimand (a confidence interval over policy value that respects the partial identification) rather than a point estimate. The thesis should adopt this honest answer rather than pretending point identification holds.

(C) Calibration drift induced by the policy. If pi systematically presents item q to high-theta students, then alpha_q and beta_q will be re-estimated on a biased subsample. Over time this corrupts the calibration in a way that compounds. The fix is either to enforce exploration in pi (Thompson sampling, UCB-style item selection) or to periodically inject random-item exposure for the explicit purpose of calibration. Both have a cost in student-perceived value and the cost-benefit is research, not engineering.

(D) Latent dynamics under intervention. Theta_s(t) changes for two reasons, learning and policy choice. A trajectory where theta rises after pi presented hard items could be either "the policy worked" or "the student was going to improve anyway and the policy happened to coincide." Disentangling these is a causal inference problem, not a measurement problem. It requires either randomized variation in pi (which deployment will not allow at scale) or a structural model of learning that is identified from the variation pi does produce (which is the harder route and probably what the thesis ends up doing).

These four problems are the substance of Thrust 3. They are not solved in standard DRL or standard psychometrics. They are the contribution of the framing.

## 6. What lives beyond the closed loop

Auditing, fairness, drift, generative items. These are not afterthoughts and they are not D3 papers. They are Thrust 4 and they are inside the thesis.

Reason. A closed loop on a corrupt bank is worse than no loop, because the policy will exploit miscalibrations the loop cannot see. So the bank-and-apparatus thrust is structurally required. The candidate cannot say "I will close the loop and someone else can audit the bank." The auditing has to be part of the same primitive.

What goes here specifically. Streaming DIF detection (does item q have systematically different difficulty across student subgroups that the policy is treating differently). Cold-start calibration for LLM-generated items (item enters the bank with prior uncertainty, gets exposed to a random sample of students under controlled exploration, achieves calibration before being used by pi). Population drift detection (the cohort changes mid-deployment, the calibration that was valid in January is not valid in June, when do you flag this). Person-fit and aberrance detection in stream (which is essentially anomaly detection on the residual stream of the measurement primitive).

Fairness deserves a paragraph of its own. In closed-loop, the policy makes decisions about who sees what. If the measurement primitive is biased on a subgroup, the policy will systematically allocate worse content to that subgroup, and that allocation will reinforce the bias because the subgroup will produce responses consistent with the biased measurement. This is a self-fulfilling prophecy with mathematical structure. The thesis cannot dodge it, and the fairness paper in Thrust 4 should be specifically about this self-reinforcement, not about generic algorithmic-fairness rebadging.

What does NOT live in the thesis. Cognitive diagnosis (DINA/DINO style discrete skill profiles) is a different measurement primitive and putting it in would dilute the framing. Linking and equating across forms is mostly a static-bank problem and is not where the streaming primitive bites hardest. CAT in the classical sense (item selection from a fixed bank for a one-shot exam) is a special case of the action primitive that does not exercise the closed-loop coupling. Mention these in the thesis introduction as the other streaming primitives that the framing implies, point at them as future work, do not write them.

## 7. Concrete sub-paper sketches

Five thrusts, three to four papers each. Method and data each in one sentence. Contribution gets a paragraph because that is what the framing is about.

### Thrust 1, Measurement primitive

**P1.1, MA-GPCM (already drafted).** Method, attention-memory backbone with separated ability pathway and GPCM head, streaming theta and item parameter recovery. Data, synthetic GPCM DGPs plus ASSISTments. Contribution. The first streaming neural psychometric model that recovers IRT parameters at EM-quality while delivering per-time, per-student ability without an offline pass. The intellectual move is to argue that streaming recovery is not just batch psychometrics done online, it is a different object whose value comes from things batch cannot do (within-session theta, new-student-on-arrival, scale to Q=2000). This paper establishes the existence of the measurement primitive that the rest of the thesis builds on. Without it, the closed-loop claim has no foundation.

**P1.2, Joint streaming calibration with selection awareness.** Method, extend the streaming primitive to update item parameters online while explicitly modeling the action selection process via inverse-propensity-weighted likelihood. Data, synthetic DGPs with non-random item selection plus a CAT-style real dataset. Contribution. Establishes that streaming joint calibration is possible without the selection bias that classical online calibration ignores. The deeper point is that batch-EM calibration on adaptively selected data is biased, and most CAT systems quietly absorb this bias. A streaming primitive that knows what the policy was doing while the data was being collected can correct it. This paper is the bridge to Thrust 2.

**P1.3, Multidimensional streaming psychometrics without a Q-matrix.** Method, extend MA-GPCM to D > 1 latent dimensions with identifiability constraints that hold under streaming updates (rotational identifiability in stream is a non-trivial problem because the basis can drift). Data, synthetic multidimensional DGPs plus a dataset with known multidimensional structure such as TIMSS. Contribution. Multidimensional IRT in batch needs a Q-matrix or strong rotational priors. In stream you do not have either at the start. The contribution is a streaming primitive that learns a meaningful multidimensional decomposition without a Q-matrix, with identifiability achieved through dynamic regularization rather than static constraints. This is theoretically the hardest paper in Thrust 1 and the one that earns the "primitive" framing because it shows the streaming object can do something batch genuinely struggles with.

**P1.4, Open-cohort streaming theta.** Method, modify the measurement primitive so new students arrive mid-stream with cold-start theta priors that warm up in O(small number) of responses. Data, simulated open cohorts with arrival rates calibrated to real platform turnover. Contribution. Closes a structural gap in classical IRT, which assumes a closed cohort. The contribution is the cold-start machinery and a characterization of how fast theta is identified for an arriving student under different exposure policies. This paper is also the natural place to connect to Thrust 2 because cold-start exposure is itself a policy choice.

### Thrust 2, Action primitive on streaming measurement

**P2.1, Bandit content recommendation conditioned on streaming psychometric state.** Method, contextual bandit (Thompson sampling) where the context is the measurement primitive's current state, with a reward defined on observable correctness and mastery-threshold crossings. Data, simulated tutoring with realistic forgetting plus a small offline evaluation on real logs. Contribution. The first action primitive that takes streaming psychometric state as input rather than a static feature vector. The contribution is not the bandit, it is the demonstration that the state representation from the measurement primitive is more sample-efficient for the policy than handcrafted state features or DKT-style next-response prediction. This paper has to be careful to credit the measurement primitive for the gain and not pretend the bandit did it.

**P2.2, Policy gradient on non-Markovian streaming state.** Method, policy gradient with the measurement primitive's hidden state as a learned representation, with explicit regularization for the policy to respect partial identifiability of the underlying ability. Data, simulated multi-week tutoring with within-session and across-session dynamics. Contribution. Where the bandit paper handles the easy case (mostly stationary, mostly Markovian under the measurement primitive's state), this paper handles the realistic case where the student forgets, gets fatigued, and re-engages. The contribution is showing that policy classes regularized by the measurement primitive's confidence bounds dominate policy classes that ignore measurement uncertainty.

**P2.3, Curriculum sequencing as long-horizon RL on streaming measurement.** Method, model-based RL with the measurement primitive as the learned world model, with horizon-bounded planning over content sequences. Data, semester-scale simulation with prerequisite structure plus a real curriculum graph from one platform. Contribution. Lifts the action primitive from per-item bandit to multi-week curriculum, which is where educational value actually lives. The contribution is the model-based formulation in which the measurement primitive plays the world-model role, and the demonstration that long-horizon planning is feasible because the world model is small and structured.

### Thrust 3, Closed-loop validity

**P3.1, Off-policy evaluation with a learned observation model.** Method, derive bounds on policy value when the reward function depends on a learned theta with non-trivial uncertainty, building on the partial-identifiability literature for reward learning [Skalse et al. 2022]. Data, synthetic with ground-truth theta so coverage can be checked. Contribution. The technical core of the thesis. Standard OPE assumes a known or correctly specified reward model. In closed-loop educational AI the reward depends on theta which depends on the data the policy generated. The contribution is a formal characterization of which counterfactuals are identified, which are partially identified with a set-valued answer, and which are not identified at all, plus an estimator that achieves nominal coverage under the partial identification. This is the paper that defends the thesis against the "you cannot evaluate anything" attack.

**P3.2, Selection-aware streaming likelihoods.** Method, develop a streaming likelihood that explicitly models adaptive item selection as part of the data-generating process, with consistency theorems under mild conditions on the policy class. Data, synthetic with various levels of policy adaptiveness, plus the candidate's MA-GPCM streaming setting under adaptive item selection. Contribution. Theoretical paper. Shows when streaming theta is consistent under adaptive selection and when it is not, and constructs a corrected likelihood for the inconsistent cases. This paper is what lets you say "the loop is closed and the measurement primitive is still honest."

**P3.3, Calibration drift detection under policy use.** Method, sequential change-point detection on per-item residuals from the streaming primitive, with controlled false-discovery rate under the closed-loop dependence. Data, simulated drift scenarios plus a real long-running dataset. Contribution. Identifies when the policy has biased calibration to the point that the loop needs to be opened (exploration injected, or item parameters frozen for re-calibration). The contribution is a principled stopping rule for closed-loop operation, which is necessary for any deployment. Without this paper, the thesis says "close the loop" without saying when to stop.

**P3.4, Causal identifiability of learning under intervention.** Method, structural model of theta dynamics under policy choice, with identification arguments from instrument-like variation produced by exploration noise in the policy. Data, simulated learning curves under various policy classes, plus an RCT-like real dataset if any can be obtained. Contribution. Disentangles policy effect from intrinsic learning in the theta trajectory. The contribution is the identification argument, not the estimator. This is the most theoretical paper in the thesis and the one that gives the thesis a claim on the causal-inference-meets-education literature. Risky paper, high reward if it lands.

### Thrust 4, Bank, apparatus, drift

**P4.1, Cold-start calibration for LLM-generated items in closed-loop.** Method, prior elicitation from item text (small LM scoring) combined with bandit-driven exposure for calibration, with safety bounds on how much exposure an uncalibrated item gets. Data, an item bank with both human-authored and LLM-generated items, real calibration outcomes. Contribution. The first treatment of LLM-item induction into a closed-loop measurement primitive. The contribution is the exposure-safety bound, which lets a deployment use generative items without exposing students to items whose difficulty is unknown. This paper is also the thesis's clearest practical relevance hook for the LLM-in-education conversation.

**P4.2, Streaming DIF without pre-specified groups.** Method, latent-group DIF detection in stream, where group structure is discovered rather than pre-specified, with control of false discovery across the implicit multiple-testing burden. Data, synthetic DIF with known structure plus a real dataset with demographic labels. Contribution. Classical DIF requires you to know the groups in advance (gender, language, ethnicity). In open cohorts and adaptive platforms, the policy-relevant subgroups are emergent (students who took prerequisite X, students who arrived in cohort Y). The contribution is a DIF primitive that finds the groups and flags the items, in stream.

**P4.3, Self-reinforcing fairness analysis in closed-loop.** Method, formal model of the policy-measurement self-reinforcement, with an estimator of long-run subgroup disparity under a fixed policy, plus exploration-based mitigation. Data, simulated cohorts with built-in subgroup heterogeneity plus a real platform if possible. Contribution. Shows that closed-loop systems have a fairness failure mode that does not exist in two-stage systems, namely that policy choices amplify measurement biases through the subgroup's own response stream. The contribution is the diagnosis, the formal model, and the mitigation. This is the paper that lets the thesis claim it took fairness seriously and not as a checkbox.

### Thrust 5, Sim-to-real and deployment

**P5.1, Faithful simulation environments for closed-loop educational AI.** Method, build a simulator that exposes the right joints (cohort opening, item bank turnover, drift, forgetting) calibrated against real platform statistics, with explicit declaration of what is cartoon and what is faithful. Data, calibration statistics from one or more real platforms. Contribution. The closed-loop thesis depends on simulation for everything that cannot be RCT'd. The contribution is not "yet another KT simulator," it is a principled declaration of which simulator dimensions are validated against reality and which are not, plus a benchmark suite that other closed-loop work can use. This paper is infrastructure but it is necessary infrastructure.

**P5.2, Deployment study, small scale.** Method, deploy the streaming primitive (without the policy, just the measurement) on a real platform for one cohort, comparing streaming theta to held-out responses and to a batch baseline. Data, one real classroom or platform partnership. Contribution. The honest first step. Shows the measurement primitive transfers from simulation to reality on the easier half of the thesis, before the policy is deployed. The contribution is the transfer result and the honest catalog of what broke.

**P5.3, Deployment study, closed-loop.** Method, deploy the closed-loop primitive (measurement plus a conservative policy) on a small cohort with held-out comparison to a non-adaptive baseline. Data, the same partnership extended. Contribution. The capstone deployment paper. Validates the closed-loop claim on real students at small scale with the conservative policy that Thrust 3 derived. The contribution is the empirical demonstration that the framing is not simulation theatre.

## 8. Venue strategy

Primary venue layer is AIED, IJAIED, EDM, LAK. These are the venues that read educational measurement as a discipline rather than as application-of-ML. The framing papers (MA-GPCM, the streaming primitive papers, the fairness paper, the deployment papers) go here.

Secondary venue layer is ML, specifically NeurIPS, ICML, AISTATS, UAI. The technical-heart papers (selection-aware streaming likelihood, OPE with learned observation model, causal identifiability under intervention) go here. These are the papers where the methodological novelty is the contribution and the educational application is the motivation. ML reviewers reward the methodological core, and these papers have one.

Specifically.

P1.1 MA-GPCM, IJAIED or AIED.
P1.2 joint streaming calibration, EDM or AIED, with a methods version for AISTATS if the selection-aware likelihood is sharp.
P1.3 multidimensional, AIED or Psychometrika (worth attempting because Psychometrika prestige matters for this framing).
P1.4 open cohort, EDM or LAK.

P2.1 bandit on psychometric state, EDM or RecSys.
P2.2 policy gradient on non-Markovian state, ICML or NeurIPS.
P2.3 curriculum RL, NeurIPS or AIED depending on whether the result lands on method or application.

P3.1 OPE with learned observation model, NeurIPS or ICML. The thesis's strongest ML paper.
P3.2 selection-aware streaming likelihood, AISTATS or Annals of Statistics if the theorems are clean enough.
P3.3 drift detection, EDM or LAK.
P3.4 causal identifiability, UAI or AISTATS, possibly Psychometrika.

P4.1 LLM item cold-start, AIED or NeurIPS Datasets and Benchmarks track.
P4.2 streaming DIF, Psychometrika or LAK.
P4.3 self-reinforcing fairness, FAccT or LAK. FAccT gives the fairness framing legibility, LAK gives the educational framing legibility. Probably FAccT first, LAK second iteration.

P5.1 simulator, EDM or NeurIPS Datasets and Benchmarks.
P5.2 deployment measurement-only, AIED or LAK.
P5.3 deployment closed-loop, IJAIED. The thesis capstone.

Two strategic notes. First, do not send the OPE paper to AIED first, even though it is part of the thesis. AIED reviewers will not have the technical context to evaluate it and a rejection there will not help the thesis. Send it to ML first, then cite it in the IJAIED capstone. Second, the FAccT paper matters disproportionately for the thesis's external legibility, because fairness work is what makes the closed-loop framing legible to readers outside the immediate field.

## 9. Risks and falsifiers

What would make this thesis collapse.

**Risk 1, Closed-loop coupling does not improve over periodic recalibration.** If a two-stage system that re-runs EM every week is empirically indistinguishable from the closed-loop primitive, the framing is rhetorical. This is the highest empirical risk and the one Thrust 2 and Thrust 5 are designed to address. Mitigation. Be explicit early about what level of dynamism is required for the gap to appear. Stationary banks and slow-drifting cohorts will not show the gap. Open cohorts, fast-drifting banks, and within-session ability tracking will.

**Risk 2, Selection-aware streaming likelihood does not have nice theoretical properties.** If consistency under adaptive selection requires conditions that no realistic policy satisfies, Thrust 3 has no foundation paper. Mitigation. Even partial results matter here, and the right move is probably to identify a useful policy class (UCB-style with bounded exploration) under which consistency holds, rather than trying to prove a universal result.

**Risk 3, OPE under learned observation model is too pessimistic.** Partial identification could yield confidence intervals so wide that no policy comparison is informative. Mitigation. The thesis can still proceed with two retreats. (a) Use the wide intervals as a stress test, and propose narrower intervals under additional assumptions that are themselves empirically defensible. (b) Use simulation with known ground truth to show that the loss from partial identification is small in practice, even if the worst-case interval is wide.

**Risk 4, Deployment access does not materialize.** Without P5.2 and P5.3, the thesis is simulation-only and the framing's credibility is damaged. Mitigation. Start partnership conversations in the first year of the thesis, not the last. Even a single classroom of N=30 for one semester is enough to validate the measurement primitive end-to-end. Closed-loop deployment is the harder ask and may have to settle for a quasi-deployment (replay against logged data with the policy off-policy evaluated).

**Risk 5, The thesis is read as five loosely connected papers.** This is the failure mode of any ambitious framing. Mitigation. The introduction has to do real work, not boilerplate. It must argue the framing in a way that makes each thrust necessary, not optional. The reader should finish the introduction unable to imagine removing any of the five thrusts without breaking the argument. If they can imagine removing one, the framing has not been earned.

**Risk 6, The candidate burns out trying to do everything.** Five thrusts with three to four papers each is fifteen to twenty papers. That is not a PhD, that is a research program. Mitigation. Many of these papers can be small. Some can be workshop papers that establish a position without full empirical validation. The thesis itself is probably three to five flagship papers (one per thrust) plus supporting workshop and short papers. The blueprint above is the research agenda, not the publication list, and the thesis should be ruthless about which papers are flagship versus supporting.

Open empirical questions that determine whether the framing holds.

(i) Is there a real-world setting where within-session ability tracking matters enough to justify the streaming primitive over batch with weekly recalibration. The thesis depends on the answer being yes. If batch-weekly is enough, the streaming primitive is research for its own sake.

(ii) Is there a policy class where closed-loop coupling demonstrably beats two-stage under honest evaluation. This is what P3.1 and P2.2 are jointly testing.

(iii) Can LLM-generated items be calibrated fast enough to be safely used in closed-loop. If not, the LLM-item story is a future-work paragraph, not a thrust contribution.

(iv) Will real deployment partners accept the conservative policy that the OPE bounds permit. If the bounds are so conservative that no partner will allow the policy to actually act, the deployment paper becomes measurement-only and the closed-loop claim retreats to simulation.

These are the four questions the candidate should be probing in year one, before committing to the full thrust structure.

## 10. Closing note on what kind of thesis this is

This is a thesis that argues for a framing, not for a method. The methods exist in service of the framing. The candidate's job is to make the case that batch educational measurement is the wrong primitive for the apparatus we have now, that the right primitive is streaming and that the streaming primitive entails coupling measurement and action. The methods (MA-GPCM, the streaming likelihoods, the OPE bounds, the policies) are the demonstration that the framing is buildable.

A method-thesis can survive a weak framing if the methods are strong enough. A framing-thesis cannot survive weak methods, because the methods are the evidence for the framing. Closed-loop educational AI is the right thesis claim for this candidate at this moment, but it is a framing-thesis and the candidate must respect what that asks of them. Every paper must contribute to the framing, not just to its own thrust. The introduction must earn the framing, not assert it. The conclusion must point at the eight or nine other batch primitives in classical psychometrics that the framing also covers, without writing those papers, because that pointing is what makes the framing a research program rather than a finished claim.

This is a thesis worth writing.
