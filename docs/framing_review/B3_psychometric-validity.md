## PART 1 — DEFECTS (ranked)

**D1. FATAL — The TIMSS ordered-threshold finding is a code artifact, and the on-disk classical calibration contradicts it.**
Evidence: the neural export path sorts step thresholds unconditionally — `kt/src/deep_irt/core/decoders.py:430` (`params["beta"] = torch.sort(...)` in `item_params_sorted`, the eval-time readout) and `kt/src/deep_irt/core/model.py:658` (`b_hat = np.sort(b_hat, axis=1)`). The likelihood trains on *unsorted* beta (decoders.py:419, "unconstrained (sorted only at eval time)"). Therefore tex 1162 ("Both designs retain ordered thresholds for every item"), tex 1168 ("no item reverses the two transitions or subsumes the partial-credit category"), and the all-"yes" Ordered column of Table `tab:app_timss_item_thresholds` (tex 1607–1637) are true by construction and carry zero evidential content. The classical GPCM calibration of the same 31 items (`kt/data/timss/timss_g8_usa_gpcm_coef.csv`) has **12/31 items with b1 > b2** — under MML, the partial-credit category is non-modal for 39% of this bank, exactly the phenomenon the neural pipeline structurally cannot display. Your own campaign record already confessed this class of artifact for the real K=3 GPCM fits (`docs/exposure_rerun_results.md`, Phase 8 R8: "the neural fits' 100%-ordered is a torch.sort EXPORT ARTIFACT, not evidence"), and the paper's own corrections section (tex 1448–1450) claims the sort was separated from interpretation. It was not, for TIMSS. Reviewer sentence: *"The central finding of Section 5.4 — that the partial-credit category remains an admissible intermediate state under both designs — is enforced by a sort in the export path and is contradicted for 12 of 31 items by the authors' own classical reference; the section's headline claim must be withdrawn or re-established from the unsorted parameterization."*

**D2. FATAL — The TIMSS section presents the paper's own disease ("stable and wrong") as its reassuring case study, while the adverse artifacts sit unreported on disk.**
Evidence: tex 1139 ("the agreement analyses showed strong support for the learned ordinal structure") and tex 1172–1176 ("the stable side of the real-data story"). Against this: (a) `kt/results/p2_v3_analysis/timss_disc_check.md` — learned TIMSS discrimination vs the classical MML alpha: Spearman **.43 SK / .42 SH**, while SH-vs-SK agree with each other at **.976**; (b) `kt/results/p2_realstudy/realstudy_table_lstm.md` — TIMSS concordance-with-MML .32/.34, truth-free slack **.81/.68**; transformer table: slack **.87/.87**; dkvmn: slack .82/.73 — against the campaign's own certified flag threshold tau = .152 (`docs/exposure_rerun_results.md`, Phase 9). The neural slope range [0.23, 0.64] (tex 1170) vs classical [0.58, 1.68] is the compression signature of the shared-path pathology affecting *both* arms. High mutual agreement + low external concordance + slack five times the flag threshold is precisely the "stable and wrong" configuration the synthetic phase 5c artifacts define — occurring in the flagship real-data study and presented as support. Reviewer sentence: *"The authors' own pre-registered diagnostic and their own classical reference both flag the TIMSS fits; reporting only the SH–SK concordance while omitting both is not an oversight a revision can paper over — the section's conclusion inverts."*

**D4. MAJOR — The real-data "SH" is not the SH the paper defines; the real-data SH≈SK parity is confounded by an undisclosed capacity change.**
Evidence: tex 846–849 defines SH as the parameter head reading the width-8 shared item embedding; the synthetic beds implement that (`kt/src/deep_irt/bench/_p2_exposure_sweep.py:153`, `EMB_DIM = 8`; toggle imports it). The real-data driver gives SH a **width-64** embedding for 2PL/GPCM (`kt/src/deep_irt/bench/_p2_realstudy.py:166-169`) and width-8 for NRM — so "SH" means three different objects across the paper. Phase 8b of your own record shows widening the shared embedding materially lifts discrimination recovery. The headline real-data parity results — beta SH~SK .998, alpha .978 (tex 1191), near-parity stability — are therefore partly a consequence of handing real-SH the capacity that synthetic-SH was denied, and the synthetic-to-real bridge ("does the synthetic gap survive on real data?") is broken by design. Reviewer sentence: *"Either run real SH at the width the synthetic conclusions are about, or re-describe the design; as it stands the paper compares a strengthened SH variant on real data to the weak SH variant in simulation and narrates the difference as a data property."*

**D5. MAJOR — Rank correlation is the only recovery currency, its numeric-use validity is never established, and the promised use-level evidence is absent.**
Three parts. (i) The design promises the fourth comparison — "whether the recovered parameters change adaptive-testing behavior" (tex 813; Table `tab:beds` row "Adaptive testing") — and the results sections contain no such experiment (the CAT block is commented out, tex 872–880); the Discussion (tex 1381–1391) quotes results that do not exist in the paper. (ii) Spearman is blind to exactly the error your own Figure `fig:scatter` displays: SH *compresses* discrimination — a scale distortion invisible to rank agreement, and the quantity that drives Fisher information, stopping rules, and scoring is the numeric slope. No linked-scale RMSE/bias (mean-sigma linking) appears anywhere; tex 860's "inspected on the linear scale when their spread is substantively relevant" is not delivered as a number. Your artifacts show why this matters: rank repair without scale repair leaves decision costs intact (retrofit rank .93, RMSE@20 unchanged; `docs/exposure_rerun_results.md` Phase 9). (iii) The beta metric itself is inflated: `kt/src/deep_irt/bench/metrics_bench.py:145` computes `b_spearman` on the **raveled (Q, K−1) matrix**, so the guaranteed between-step spread (truth = sorted N(0,1) order statistics, `datagen.py:118`; estimate = sorted export, D1) contributes rank variance that no model has to earn. Within-step (per-column) Spearman is the honest number and will be lower everywhere. Reviewer sentence: *"For parameters whose use is numeric, rank recovery is a necessary-condition screen, not a validity currency; report linked-scale error and within-step threshold recovery, and either restore the downstream simulation or delete the promise."*

**D6. MAJOR — The disattenuated person-agreement values (.59/.63) in the main table are a misapplication, by the standards of the authors' own metric ruling.**
Evidence: `kt/results/p2_v3_arm1r/theta_cross_reading.json` — SH: observed .178 disattenuated by reliabilities **.245 and .373** (i.e., divided by .302), each estimated from **five seeds**, on 1,501/2,000 fingerprint-matched learners. Three technical faults: (a) cross-seed reproducibility captures optimization variance only; both readings are fit to the *same response matrix*, so response-sampling error is shared across readings — shared error inflates the observed correlation while appearing in neither reliability, biasing the corrected value upward; (b) seed-to-seed disagreement in a symmetric nominal likelihood is partly *structure* (multiple admissible optima), not error — treating it as unreliability over-corrects, the exact abuse flagged in the authors' own ruling (`kt/results/p2_v3_analysis/theta_metric_ruling.md`, Q2: "this is where a reviewer will look for cherry-picking"); (c) with a denominator of .30 estimated from n=5, the sampling error of the corrected value spans roughly [.3, >1]. The ruling mandates: observed rho as headline, one *labeled* ceiling, both caveats, rank-approximation label. The main table (tex 1252) prints the corrected values in the table body with a citation-only note. Reviewer sentence: *"Move the disattenuated values out of the table, label them as an optimization-noise ceiling under a stated independence assumption they do not fully meet, and report the reliability inputs — .245 will not survive being seen."*

## PART 2 — WHAT SURVIVES

Stated as I would accept them at a measurement venue.

1. **The prediction–recovery dissociation under correct specification (the core result) is real and well-evidenced.** At matched next-response accuracy (deltas ≤ ~4pp, never materially favoring SH), the item-to-parameter path moves discrimination rank recovery by +0.15 to +0.46 (reference cohort: 2PL LSTM .553→.898, transformer .373→.806; GPCM transformer .438→.900; Table `tab:mass`), sign-consistent across all 5 generating datasets and all 32 cells, robust to seed-clustered resampling, replicated at four N and two geometries, with a coherent mechanism (the pooled parameter lags whichever it is — discrimination for LSTM/transformer, difficulty for DKVMN). Scope: unidimensional *static* theta, lognormal(0, 0.3) discriminations, correctly specified fitted family, exactly uniform exposure, K≤4, N≤5000. Within that scope this is a solid, publishable finding.
2. **The honest ceiling statement.** Classical MML dominates both neural arms at every tested cell (.94–.98 vs SK's .91–.96 at matched exposure); SK closes most, not all, of the gap. The paper's Discussion currently says this correctly; keep it.
3. **Real-data reliability gains for SK slopes on the LSTM and DKVMN 2PL cells** (split-half .755→.847 EdNet, .748→.802 KDD; DKVMN .795→.839) — as *reliability only*, with the transformer reversal reported alongside. Stability and cross-design agreement on real data establish consistency of the estimator; they cannot establish that the parameters mean what their names claim, and the paper's own stability artifact says exactly this (`stability_shsk.md`: "smoothing tends to inflate consistency"). Used within those limits, the numbers stand.
4. **The item-location map is the robust readout everywhere.** Beta/keyed-intercept orderings agree across designs (.998), across readings (.82–.83), with empirical p-values (−.975), and with MML (.73) — location is recoverable through pooling on real data; discrimination is the fragile axis in every bed, synthetic and real. That asymmetry is a genuine, defensible psychometric finding of this paper.
5. **The TIMSS null, correctly framed, is informative but modest.** With ~5,000 learners on 31 items, per-item exposure sits in the regime where your own exposure law predicts SH≈SK — the null *confirms the synthetic exposure surface* rather than adding independent evidence. It is not underpowered (the SH–SK item agreement is .967–.976 on n=31; precision is not the issue); the issue is that both arms agree while only moderately concording with the classical calibration. Claimable: "at operational exposure the path choice is immaterial to the fitted ordinal structure." Not claimable: any validity statement about that structure.
6. **The EdNet person-side divergence at near-identical item maps** (final-theta agreement .18–.33 across readings; .21 SH–SK trajectory agreement on 2PL) is a genuinely interesting instability finding — currently under-exploited because it is dressed as an agreement result rather than reported as the sharpest real-data symptom the paper owns.

On the board's misspecification question: my judgment is that the *direction* of the SK repair survives mild misspecification (the real-data reliability gains and the DKVMN replication argue for it), but the +0.3–0.5 magnitude is a correct-specification best case, and the claim currently has **zero** robustness evidence. The single violation most likely to *reverse* it is **local dependence**: the wide item key is unregularized per-item capacity, free to absorb dependence structure into alpha, while SH's shared bottleneck acts as implicit shrinkage — and the empirical precedent already exists in your artifacts (any wide-key decoupling craters real EdNet nominal slope reliability to .065 while shared survives at .695; transformer real EdNet 2PL reverses outright). The violation most likely to *shrink* it is **drifting theta** — the KT-central condition — since the theta-noise term of your own ladder already doubles at low N under static truth, and drift inflates it for both arms, eating the channel share SK repairs. Threshold disorder, separately, voids the beta/ordinal-structure claims outright given D1.
