## Where the thread breaks, by the three named joints

**GPCM -> NRM joint.** Handled well in theory (line 251, quoted above): one shared architectural question unifies all three decoders, and 3.3's parameter-vulnerability argument (discrimination weak near the operating point; NRM's extra incidental-parameter pathway) is picked up quantitatively in Table `tab:mass` and cross-referenced once (line 1004). Handled poorly at the case-study level: GPCM's case study is TIMSS, NRM's is EdNet, and the paper never states a combined thesis for why "ordered constructed response" and "nominal option selection" belong in the same paper beyond "both are real, non-synthetic response data." The Conclusion lists their results side by side (lines 1492-1495) without a unifying sentence. One specific theoretical claim is asserted at length but never isolated empirically: the NRM's gradient-routing intervention (Eq. 30-36, the single largest derivation in the paper) is applied uniformly to every NRM result shown, so its own causal contribution (routed vs. unrouted) is argued, not demonstrated, anywhere in the current draft.

**TIMSS -> EdNet joint.** The only bridge sentence sits at the end of the *prior* section (4.3, "These results define the role of the two real-data studies that follow...", lines 1122-1124), not at the actual boundary between 4.4 and 4.5. At that boundary itself, the only connective material is "Thus whether response structures show universal agreement is dataset-specific" (line 1176), a hedge that names a fact pattern (TIMSS agrees, EdNet doesn't) without saying it. This is the most literal confirmation of the author's own diagnosis.

**Theory -> experiment joints.** Two clean hits: the 2PL/GPCM/NRM unifying question (line 251) is picked up by the shared SH/SK grid (Table `tab:mass`); the Fisher-information discrimination-weakness argument (Eqs. 21-24) is picked up and explicitly cited back (line 1004). Two clean misses, both structural rather than just narrative: the "estimator ladder" (`\ref{sec:diagnostics}`) that Section 3's amortization theory is built to motivate has no corresponding results anywhere; the ability-recovery metric that Section 4.1 spends a full paragraph specifying (Pearson correlation at last observed step, lines 862-864, itself the subject of one of the five "corrections" in 5.2) is never reported in any table or figure in the entire document (`fig_surface.pdf`, presumably this exact plot, exists on disk unused). ## The implicit storyline, read generously (5 sentences)

Knowledge-tracing models with IRT-flavored response heads are trained only to predict the next response, and this paper asks whether the ability and item variables those heads expose can be trusted anyway. In synthetic data with known ground truth, the standard design, where the parameter head reads the same item embedding the encoder uses for prediction, predicts fine but systematically under-recovers item discrimination and option-slope parameters, while giving the parameter head its own dedicated item key repairs this at essentially no predictive cost, because gradient analysis shows the shared embedding's parameter signal is diluted by a competing prediction signal exactly where that signal is weakest. Having established that this design choice is invisible to accuracy but visible to recovery, the paper turns to real data, where recovery cannot be checked, and instead asks a more modest question: do these heads stay good predictors, and do they produce response structure, partial-credit curves on TIMSS, option-selection curves on EdNet, that a reader could actually use, finding that they do, with the shared-versus-separated choice now trading prediction against external agreement rather than one design simply winning. The throughline the draft wants to tell is "accuracy is not validity, and the fix is cheap, and here is what it costs you if you skip it," but the version of that argument printed on the page only fully delivers the first clause; the mechanism-revealing middle claim (the model already has the right information, a per-item refit proves it) and the closing cost claim (an adaptive test pays for the gap in length and cut-score errors) are stated as settled facts in the abstract, introduction, discussion, and conclusion without a single supporting table or figure anywhere in the body or appendix. That gap is not a hidden design flaw needing new experiments; the refit and CAT results were run, their figures (`fig_ladder.pdf`, `fig_cat.pdf`, `fig_surface.pdf`) sit rendered on disk, and an earlier complete sibling draft (`overleaf-sync/submission/main_caeai.tex`) shows exactly how they used to close the story before the TIMSS and EdNet material was grafted on in their place.
