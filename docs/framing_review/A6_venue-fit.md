# Lens A6: venue-fit (framing review, 2026-07-17)

## Venue pattern verdict (CAEAI and siblings)

**1. What carries the storyline.** Across CAEAI, IEEE TLT, and JEDM/AIED-EDM audit-type papers, the storyline is carried by a caution paired with a tool, not by either alone. The recurring anatomy is five moves. Name a practice already in use (a system reads ability, difficulty, or mastery off a model). Show a hidden failure in that practice (the readout passes the checks a practitioner would normally run, yet is wrong). Attach a concrete decision cost to the failure (a longer test, a wrong pass or fail call). Offer a cheap, checkable instrument or fix (something a reader can run on their own model). Close with practice guidance. A paper that only states an education problem reads as descriptive for CAEAI's algorithmic scope. A paper that only offers a tool reads as a methods contribution that happened to touch education data, and belongs more naturally at JEDM or an AIED/EDM proceedings track, where methods-first framing with a thin applied payoff is normal. This project's own venue notes already found the right comparison class for this paper (`docs/paper_plan_v2.md:202-206`, citing a TLT 2024 audit-plus-repair KT paper as the nearest sibling), which confirms the shape independently of my read.

**2. How psychometric vocabulary plus deep KT must position.** The move is not to explain IRT terms more. It is to subordinate them once, cleanly, and then state the consequence in terms a KT audience already owns. The draft already does the subordination correctly in one sentence: "IRT is not an offline calibration procedure but an interpretable decoder family embedded inside a predictive sequence model" (`main_caeai.tex:73`). What is missing is the partner move: naming what a system or a person gets wrong when that decoder is unfaithful. Right now the loop never closes; the abstract introduces the decoder framing (line 46) but never returns to it as a stake.

**3. What the abstract/intro/conclusion currently match, and what they should match.** Read end to end, the current text matches an architecture-ablation and validity-metric paper, the JEDM-native shape, not the caution-plus-tool shape CAEAI rewards. Evidence:

- The pivot sentence "Knowledge tracing (KT) provides the sequence-modeling setting for this question" (`main_caeai.tex:68`) makes KT instrumental to an interpretability question rather than the thing that matters. That inverts the required emphasis.
- The three contributions (`main_caeai.tex:89-93`) are ordered validity-formulation, mechanism-localization, downstream-effect, with the only applied claim listed and weighted last, in one clause with no magnitude ("increases test length and cut-score errors").
- No named stakeholder appears anywhere in the abstract, intro, or conclusion. I checked directly for teacher, instructor, dashboard, deploy, practitioner, classroom; the only hits in that span are "downstream decisions" (63) and "deployment implications" (95), both abstract nouns, never a person or system whose output changes.
- The working title comment itself, "On the Prediction-Recovery Trade-off in Interpretable Knowledge Tracing" (`main_caeai.tex:2`), names a trade-off/characterization paper, not a caution-plus-tool paper. Compare the plan's own title slate, built around disease-and-invoice language (`docs/paper_plan_v2.md:10-19, 65-76`).

There is a mechanical reason this gap exists, and it is worth flagging as the probable single largest cause of "glued together." The abstract's only stakes claim, "In adaptive-testing simulations, the same gap increases test length and cut-score error" (`main_caeai.tex:51`), and the third contribution restating it (line 93), are not currently supported anywhere in the Experiments section. The methods paragraph for the adaptive-testing simulation is commented out in the source (`main_caeai.tex:872-880`, every line prefixed `%`). No subsection presents adaptive-testing results anywhere between the Experiments header (804) and Discussion (1370); the only subsections are Experimental Design, Synthetic Recovery, Real-Data Response Structure, Ordinal KT with Partial-Credit Responses, and Align Correctness and Option Structure. Yet the Discussion cites `Section~\ref{sec:downstream}` (line 1382) as if it exists, and states SH/SK CAT costs as settled fact (1385-1391), and both Discussion and Conclusion headers carry a literal `[FULL-REWORK]` tag. The results themselves are real and finished (`docs/exposure_rerun_results.md:361-382`: shared-head test length 196.8% of the oracle length, 95% CI [190,204], roughly double; decoupled/SK 157.2% [151,163]; +2.3 percentage points misclassification for shared at a fixed cut and length, reduced to roughly +0.6 to +0.8pp under decoupling). They are banked, seed-clustered, and cited in the plan as done. They are simply not written into the manuscript's own Experiments section. A reviewer who reads the abstract's one practical claim and goes looking for it in the body will find nothing. That is a structural hole, not a framing problem, and it is cheap to fix because the numbers already exist; it requires restoring a results subsection, not new experiments.

A second, smaller cause sits in the same span. TIMSS (ordered, GPCM) and EdNet (nominal, NRM) are never differentiated in the abstract or intro as playing different roles, yet the Discussion later reveals a real qualification: TIMSS is called "the stable side of the real-data story" (1172), implying an unstable side, and on EdNet the paper's preferred fix loses ("SH edges SK on EdNet--NRM under all three encoders," 1099), so the Discussion has to caution "The EdNet result should not be interpreted as a SK-SH ranking" (1363). Nothing in the abstract or intro foreshadows that the fix helps for ordered responses but not clearly for nominal ones. Unforeshadowed, it reads as a contradiction bolted onto the main claim rather than an honest, informative boundary. Foreshadowed, it becomes part of the practice guidance (state exactly when the fix applies).

**4. What "so what" means to education-facing reviewers here.** It means a decision, not a metric. Not "our recovery correlation is higher" but "a system that reuses this model's parameters would make a different, worse decision, by this much, and here is what to do about it without re-collecting data." The paper's own frozen campaign already produced exactly this shape of result, in the adaptive-testing simulation; it is the only place in the whole campaign where "ability" and "item parameter" cash out as something a student or an assessment system actually experiences (test length, pass/fail-style classification), which is as close as this paper gets to "assessment reuse of KT models" and "a system reporting ability." It is presently absent from the manuscript, not absent from the evidence.

## Deliverable

**Venue pattern verdict.** The abstract, intro, and conclusion of `main_caeai.tex` currently read as an architecture-ablation and validity-metric paper (compare two item-to-parameter designs, show one recovers better, localize the mechanism), which is the JEDM/AIED-EDM native shape. CAEAI-family papers on interpretability and validity succeed with a caution-plus-tool shape instead, naming a practice, a hidden failure, a decision cost, and a cheap fix, in that order, with the technical machinery subordinate throughout. This project's own plan of record already designed that exact shape (`docs/paper_plan_v2.md`, the "Disease, Cause, Invoice, Instruments, Choice" architecture, status noted as "Merchandised architecture drafted (v2.1)... then prose," line 214-216); it was never carried into this draft's abstract, intro, or conclusion, and the one section that would carry the practitioner stake (the adaptive-testing results) is currently missing from the manuscript body even though the abstract promises it and the results already exist. The fix is a rewrite plus a restoration, not a new experimental program.

**So what, honestly claimable from existing results.** A knowledge-tracing model whose IRT-parameterized head passes every check a practitioner would normally run, next-response accuracy and stability, can still be misestimating item difficulty and discrimination badly enough that, when those same estimates are reused to run an adaptive test, the simulated test runs roughly twice as long as it would under faithful parameters (196.8% of the oracle stopping length, 95% CI 190 to 204%) and produces a materially higher rate of wrong pass or fail-style classifications at a fixed test length (about 2.3 percentage points more), and a change that costs nothing at inference time, giving the parameter head its own dedicated item representation instead of sharing one with the predictor, recovers roughly half of that length inflation and most of the misclassification cost. That is the stake CAEAI reviewers would recognize: not that a recovery number went up, but that a system reusing this model's parameters for adaptive testing or item banking would quietly test students longer and misclassify some of them, in simulation on generated item banks rather than a live deployment, and the paper both shows why (an amortization path, not a data limit) and shows a cheap partial fix. The paper does not currently make this claim, with these numbers, anywhere in its abstract, intro, or conclusion, and the results section that would support it is not currently in the manuscript.

**Cost note.** Everything above is a write-up and restoration cost against already-completed, banked results (`docs/exposure_rerun_results.md`), consistent with the frozen-campaign constraint. It is not a request for new experiments. A further extension, such as a real-data (not simulated-examinee) replication of the adaptive-testing cost, or a worked example tied to a named platform-reporting scenario, would strengthen the stake further but is genuinely new-experiment-scale work and should be flagged as optional, not required to close the narrative gap identified here.
