# DEFECTS (ranked)

## FATAL

**F1 — The one claim that separates this from a trivial capacity effect is asserted, not shown.**
The synthetic SH-vs-SK gap in `tab:mass` (main headline, e.g. LSTM-2PL discrimination .553→.898) confounds representation-sharing with item-side capacity. Per the paper (lines 846-849) SH reads a width-8 embedding; SK reads a dedicated width-64 key plus its own Q×64 table and a wider readout map — roughly +12.8k item-side params at Q=200. `tab:mass` compares these two arms with no capacity-matched control anywhere in the results (804-1367) or appendix. The load-bearing sentence "the gap is therefore an item-wise amortization error rather than a data or capacity limit" (line 50) has no supporting figure. The deconfounding controls exist off-paper only — the hyperparameter table names them (lines 1708-1709, "Narrow-key capacity control item key 16", "Widened-embedding control shared width 16 to 96") and the design doc reports them (Phase 8/8b: widen SH 8→96 plateaus +0.056 *below* decoupled at 49k vs 21k params; SK key-16 still beats SH). Tellingly, the authors capacity-match on *real* data (`_p2_realstudy.py:167` shared 2PL/GPCM `emb_dim=64`) but not on the *synthetic* grid where the headline gap lives. A reviewer would write: "As presented I cannot distinguish 'sharing degrades the head' from 'the head needs more parameters.' Promote the width-sweep and narrow-key controls into the main results; until then the central claim is unsupported."

**F2 — The downstream adaptive-testing harm is a headline with zero in-paper evidence.**
"In adaptive-testing simulations, the same gap increases test length and cut-score error" (line 51, repeated as a contribution at 93 and throughout the discussion 1381-1425 and conclusion 1511). There is no CAT table or figure. The design paragraph is commented out (872-880), yet `tab:beds` advertises an "Adaptive testing" component (922-925) and the hyperparameter table lists CAT settings (1719-1723). The evidence exists off-paper (design doc Phase 7/11, `p2_cat`) but the manuscript ships the claim with nothing behind it. Reviewer: "The third contribution is not evidenced by the paper's own results."

**F3 — The mechanistic core (gap localization via an estimator ladder) points at a section that does not exist.**
"We trace the gap to this shared path" (86); "the estimator ladder of Section~\ref{sec:diagnostics}" (811, 851); "The estimator ladder localizes much of this loss to the amortized channel" (1504). `sec:diagnostics` is a dangling reference — there is no such section and no AM/RF/MML ladder table in the results. The oracle-ladder decomposition (channel ≈0.22 vs theta-noise ≈0.05, design Phase 5a, `p2_oracle`) is exactly the argument that would make the amortization claim mechanistic, and it is absent.

*(F1-F3 are the same disease: the intro/abstract/discussion assert three results — not-capacity, downstream-CAT, gap-localization — that the current results sections do not contain. The evidence is on disk; the paper does not show it.)*

## MAJOR

**M1 — The NRM-over-direct "large gains" are gains over a baseline that loses to per-item majority.** *(board Q3)*
Lines 1093-1096 headline "Every NRM head improves selected-option prediction over the corresponding direct predictor. The gains are large" (LSTM .526→.648). But the item-wise popular-option baseline is **.653** (appendix line 1680; text 1275-1283) — above *every* NRM head (.643-.648) and *every* direct predictor (.526-.565). So the "+.08-.12 win" measures the direct MLP's failure to learn item-conditional base rates, not a virtue of the NRM head. The NRM's edge is its explicit per-item intercept c_k, which correlates .78-.85 with raw option frequency (appendix 1671) — an expected base-rate inductive bias. Fairness check passes but cuts against the claim: the direct NRM predictor uses `emb_dim=64` (`_p2_real_prediction_baselines.py:56`) versus the NRM heads' `emb_dim=8` (`_p2_realstudy.py:172-174`), so it has *more* item capacity and still underfits base rates; both train 120 epochs, LR .01, no early stopping — equal and untuned. The claim "structured heads are not merely post-hoc interpretability layers" (1093) is not supported by a top-1 number that no method pushes past a per-item majority vote. Reviewer: "Put the .653 majority baseline in Table 3, not the appendix, and reframe. The honest statement is that nothing beats per-item majority on top-1; the NRM's gain over direct is a base-rate effect."

**M2 — The central selectivity story ("locations robust, discriminations fragile") is contradicted by the paper's own DKVMN rows.** *(board Q1)*
The narrative (976-980, 999-1004) and the Fisher-geometry argument (660-667) assert discrimination is the vulnerable family. For DKVMN this inverts: in `tab:mass` DKVMN-2PL SH shows α=.752 but β=.652 (difficulty is worse), and the source artifact labels DKVMN's hard parameter "b (difficulty)" (`mass_table.md:37-44`). The paper's blanket "for the ordered heads, this is discrimination" (977) is false for one of its three encoders. The correct and more interesting statement (design Phase 10) is encoder-conditional: whichever parameter stays pooled is the laggard, and DKVMN's memory already gives items private slots so its pooled laggard is difficulty. Hiding this both breaks the clean story and forfeits the one genuinely general finding. Reviewer: "State the encoder dependence; your own Table 1 refutes the universal-discrimination framing."

**M3 — The strongest-looking real-data cells are the least replicated.**
`tab:real_prediction` note (1082-1084) concedes DKVMN uses 3-6 folds, one seed. The underlying artifact is worse: `realdirect_table.md:22,28-29` shows dkvmn-direct n=3, dkvmn IRT-shared n=1 on TIMSS/KDD/NRM, dkvmn IRT-separate n=1 across all four datasets (NRM later lifted to n=5 in `p2_v3_arm1r`, single seed). The specific sentences "DKVMN-SK reaches 0.645 on EdNet-2PL matching MML" (1090) and the TIMSS DKVMN-SK .584/.588 (1074) rest on 1-5 single-seed folds — no seed-clustering is even possible. Reviewer: "A one-fold cell cannot support 'matches the classical reference.'"

**M4 — MML EdNet-NRM is shown on a favorable 34% subset.** `tab:real_prediction` lists MML EdNet-NRM = .609† "covered positions only"; the full-panel score is **.279** at 0.34 coverage (`realdirect_table.md:8,46`). Classical calibration fails on two-thirds of the nominal bank; placing .609 in-column beside neural .643-.648 on the full panel flatters it. The dagger discloses but does not correct the visual comparison.

# WHAT SURVIVES (as I would accept it at a DL venue)

**Defensible core.** On a well-specified synthetic DGP with known generating parameters, a prediction-trained IRT readout whose item head is amortized from the sequence encoder's own item embedding recovers the *rank* of the weakly-identified parameter family poorly (Spearman ≈ .37-.75 at the reference cohort), while giving that head a dedicated embedding raises recovery to ≈ .81-.96 at ≤1pp change in next-response accuracy. 25 fits/cell, 5 seeds, clustered CIs, 32/32 cells positive with 5/5 sign agreement (design R-hardening). Honest effect size +.16 to +.50 discrimination-rho, largest for the transformer. Critically it does *not* close with 10× more learners (shared 2PL-LSTM N500→5000 .37→.61 vs decoupled →.95). Recovery and prediction are separable, and the shortfall behaves like an identifiability/amortization effect *conditional on the capacity control being added*.

**Also survives.** A 2PL/GPCM bottleneck head costs no measurable accuracy versus a same-encoder direct predictor on EdNet/KDD/TIMSS (within ±1-2pp). TIMSS SH≈SK is a clean stability negative (thresholds ordered 31/31 items both designs, expected-score maps agree, trajectory median r≈.84). The genuinely general and currently under-stated law: under a shared embedding, whichever item-parameter group stays pooled is the one that fails to recover and does not benefit from scale; an own-channel fixes it — encoder-conditional across three encoders and two geometries.

**Board Q1 — would a practitioner predict it?** Qualitatively yes. Shared-trunk-degrades-an-unsupervised-auxiliary-readout plus separate-head-fixes-it is multi-task interference / amortization-gap (Cremer 2018) / linear-probe-vs-finetune folklore; the "fix" is the standard separate-head-with-stop-gradient. The non-obvious residuals are (i) selectivity by parameter *identifiability* and (ii) non-convergence with data scale. Both are IRT-flavored, not general ML. The zero prediction cost is fully expected (the encoder keeps its own embedding; the head's key is pure addition). The magnitude (discrimination rho .37 at healthy accuracy) is the one genuinely striking number.

**Board Q2 — is the two-resolution EdNet analysis a novel ML template?** No, routine multi-view agreement in domain dress. Reading the same items/learners under two response heads and correlating parameters by family, with fingerprint-matched learners (1,501/2,000) and Spearman-1904 disattenuation using cross-seed reproducibility as reliability, is cross-view representational agreement (RSA / CKA / model-stitching lineage) plus a classical reliability correction. Competent, but not a new interpretability method for an ML audience.

**Board Q4 — strongest honest novelty sentence and ICLR score.** Sentence: "Amortizing an interpretable low-dimensional parameter head from the same embedding a sequence model optimizes for prediction leaves the weakly-identified parameter directions unrecovered (rank-correlation as low as .37 at unchanged accuracy) and no amount of additional data closes the gap, whereas a head with its own embedding recovers them — identifying the shortfall as a multi-task amortization/identifiability effect rather than a capacity or sample-size limit." Score as-is: **3 (reject), borderline 3-4.** Known mechanism, expected fix; the one ML-general residual (scale does not cure it) is real but under-emphasized and confounded with capacity by the missing control (F1); three headline claims carry no in-paper evidence (F1-F3); the lone positive-looking ML result (NRM > direct) is against a sub-trivial baseline (M1). This is a solid CAEAI/ed-AI paper where the measurement framing carries it, not an ICLR methods contribution.

# THE ONE DEMAND

**Run the misspecification-robustness experiment.** The entire thesis — prediction can be right while parameters are wrong, and the wrongness is architectural — is established only on a self-consistent simulator whose model family equals the generating family, and the real-data legs cannot adjudicate recovery (no ground truth). The 7-violation probe (local dependence, noisy thresholds, response style, threshold disorder, DIF, exposure imbalance, drifting theta) was **built and never run** — confirmed: `run_misspecification_probe.py` and its test exist, no outputs anywhere on disk. Under misspecification the result could persist, shrink to nothing (both heads equally wrong, decoupling buys nothing), or invert (the prediction-shaped shared embedding could be the *more* robust one). This is the single outcome that would move my verdict.

Design: reference cohort (N=2000, Q=200), LSTM and transformer, 2PL and GPCM; inject each violation at a clean control plus 2-3 doses, 5 seeds. Pre-register two estimands as dose-response curves — (a) the SH→SK recovery delta (Spearman) per violation per dose, and (b) whether the truth-free slack diagnostic still tracks the now-degraded truth. Reporting the recovery-delta × dose curves converts the claim from "amortization gap on a clean simulator" to "amortization gap that survives realistic misspecification." Ship this and F1's capacity controls in the same table and the synthetic core becomes genuinely defensible; without them it is a careful study of a simulator.
