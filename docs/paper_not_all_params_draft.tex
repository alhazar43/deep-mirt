\title{Not All Parameters Learn Alike: Uneven Recovery of Interpretable Parameters in Prediction-Trained Knowledge Tracing}

\author{Wenrui Yuan}
\address{Section Cognition, Data and Education, University of Twente, The Netherlands}

\begin{abstract}
Knowledge tracing (KT) models increasingly attach an item-response-theory (IRT) readout to a sequence encoder so that a next-response prediction can also be read as a student ability, an item discrimination, and a set of step thresholds. Whether those readouts are recovered, as opposed to merely present, is rarely audited. We treat recovery as the object of study. We first show that the encoder and the IRT decoder are swappable modular slots rather than the fixed pairing prior work assumed, and that a standard prediction loss recovers the readout parameters without any variational machinery. On synthetic data with known generating parameters, recovery is real but uneven, and the unevenness follows a stable order in which discrimination returns last. A local analysis of the readout under gradient flow explains the order. Each coordinate relaxes at a rate set by its Fisher information, and discrimination is the flat direction because its information is suppressed where responses concentrate. This rate penalty is a finite-budget effect that closes as data and training grow, so it bites in the realistic regime rather than in the limit. A separate and structural failure occurs when the ability readout is allowed to see the current item, which breaks the ability-difficulty location split at every budget. Embedding and readout configuration is the lever. A decoupled embedding, a narrow ability input with a separate wide item key, escapes the shared-capacity trade-off and restores the location split; a state-conditioned discrimination head instead helps only a low-information parameter and can hurt one that is not. The nominal response model, whose slope loads on ability but is not low in information, separates the two effects. On real educational data we report the honest mixed verdict, that a static decoupled configuration is the reliability winner and that the decoupling benefit is contingent on item coverage. The current evidence is synthetic recovery plus real-data reliability; a rigorous rebuild under one protocol and a downstream adaptive-testing evaluation are the planned strengthening steps.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Knowledge tracing predicts a student's next response from the history of past interactions, and the field has moved from hidden Markov models \citep{corbett_knowledge_1994} to deep sequence models \citep{piech_deep_2015, zhang_dynamic_2017, pandey_self-attentive_2019, ghosh_context-aware_2020}. Those sequence models predict well, but the states they learn are high-dimensional and not, by construction, tied to quantities a teacher or a test designer can act on. A now-common response is to attach an item-response-theory (IRT) readout to the encoder, so that the same forward pass that predicts a response also emits an ability estimate, an item discrimination, and item difficulty \citep{yeung_deep-irt_2019, converse_irt_kt_2021, xu_dkvmn_2024}. The appeal is interpretability. The hidden state is read as ability, and the item embedding is read as discrimination and difficulty.

This paper flips the premise that motivates that construction. The IRT readouts are asserted, or checked against face validity, far more often than they are audited against a known ground truth. We therefore ask a plain question. When a KT encoder with an IRT readout is trained only to predict responses, does it actually recover the readout parameters it exposes? To ask the question cleanly we need a framework whose encoder and decoder are genuinely separable, so that any answer is a property of the readout and the training signal rather than of one architecture. We build such a framework, and we find that on synthetic data with known generating parameters recovery is real, but it is uneven, and the unevenness follows a stable order across encoder and decoder swaps. Item difficulty and student ability come back quickly and reliably. Item discrimination comes back last, least reliably, and systematically under-recovered.

The order is not an accident of any one encoder, and a local analysis of the readout explains it. Near an optimum, each readout coordinate relaxes under gradient flow at a rate set by that coordinate's Fisher information, and discrimination is the slow direction because its information, which scales with the squared separation between ability and difficulty, is suppressed exactly where the responses concentrate. Stated loudly, this rate penalty is a finite-budget property. It closes as the number of training steps and the number of observations grow, so it is a genuine feature of the realistic regime in which practitioners train, not an asymptotic impossibility. A second failure is different in kind. If the ability readout is allowed to condition on the current item, ability can absorb that item's location, and the split between ability and difficulty is no longer identified. This one is structural. It does not vanish with more data, because it is a property of what the loss can and cannot pin down.

Both failures are addressed by how the embedding and the readout are wired, and there are two distinct levers that must not be confused. A decoupled embedding, meaning a narrow ability input together with a separate wide item key, escapes the shared-capacity trade-off between ability and discrimination and, applied to the ability readout, restores the location split. This lever is about representation, and it fires whenever a readout shares a representation with ability, whether or not that readout is low in information. A state-conditioned, or dynamic, discrimination head is the other lever. It feeds the learner state into the discrimination readout, and it helps a low-information parameter while it can hurt a parameter that is not low in information. To keep the two levers apart we use the nominal response model (NRM) as a control, because its category slope loads on ability like discrimination but carries near-symmetric information. On the NRM the representation lever still fires and the dynamic lever reverses, which is exactly what tells the two apart.

We summarize the contribution as five points.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The encoder and the decoder are swappable modular slots.} The interpretable pairing that prior work treated as a fixed architecture is in fact a sequence encoder slot and an IRT readout slot that vary independently. We demonstrate the pairing across encoders and decoders. This is the instrument, not the claim.
    \item \textbf{Plain prediction loss suffices.} Prior neural-IRT recovers parameters through variational inference \citep{wu_variational_2020}. We show that standard prediction-loss KT training recovers them too, with no variational posterior and no evidence lower bound. This both simplifies the recipe and sets up the catch.
    \item \textbf{Some parameters recover badly, and it is a finite-data problem.} A local gradient-flow analysis says why. Low Fisher information makes discrimination the slow direction, and coupling the ability readout to the item breaks the location split. Experiments say how, through a stable recovery order. Most of the rate penalty vanishes as data and training grow, so the contribution lives in the realistic finite regime.
    \item \textbf{Two levers, and which wins is genuinely mixed.} On synthetic recovery a dynamic head can rescue the low-information discrimination channel. On real-data reliability the winner is a static decoupled configuration. We report the mix straight.
    \item \textbf{The nominal response model explains the mix.} Its slope loads on ability but is not low in information, so it separates a representation effect, which is robust and fires regardless of information, from a Fisher effect, which is conditional and helps only a low-information parameter. A static decoupled configuration winning on finite real data is therefore a prediction of the theory, not an embarrassment.
\end{enumerate}

The single thread through the paper is to change the configuration and watch recovery move. KT nets bolt on an IRT readout and declare interpretability done. We ask whether the readout is recovered. To ask cleanly we build a framework whose encoder and decoder swap. On synthetic ground truth recovery is real but uneven and stable across swaps, with plain prediction loss and no variational machinery. A local analysis of the readout explains the unevenness as a finite-budget rate effect plus a structural identifiability effect. Configuration is the lever, decoupling for the representation problem and a dynamic head for the low-information problem, with the NRM as the control that separates them. The pattern holds on real data, where the honest verdict is a static decoupled configuration. We describe the downstream decision this would change, adaptive item selection, as a planned evaluation.

Throughout, the home is knowledge tracing and the representation learning inside a KT encoder. IRT supplies readable coordinates and a controlled recovery target, not the subject. We do not claim a new psychometric result. The contribution is the translation of a classical fact about information geometry into the training dynamics of a prediction-trained tracer, and the practical levers that follow.

\section{A Swappable Knowledge-Tracing Framework}
\label{sec:framework}
The instrument for the whole study is a framework that separates a KT model into two slots, a sequence encoder and an IRT-style readout, that can be varied independently. This section defines the two slots and their interface, and it presents the memory-augmented instance that motivated the question and that we reuse as the precursor.

\subsection{The Two Slots and Their Interface}
\label{sec:two_slots}
At time step $t$ a student interacts with an item indexed by $i$ and produces a response $r_t \in \{0, 1, \ldots, K-1\}$ over $K$ ordered categories, with $K=2$ the binary correct-or-incorrect special case. Writing the interaction as $x_t = (i_t, r_t)$ and the history as $\mathcal{X}_{t} = \{x_1, \ldots, x_t\}$, the prediction target is the distribution of the next response,
\begin{equation}
    P\bigl(r_{t+1} = k \mid i_{t+1}, \mathcal{X}_t \bigr), \qquad k \in \{0, \ldots, K-1\}.
\end{equation}

The \emph{encoder} is any causal sequence model that consumes the encoded history and emits a state, $h_t = \mathrm{enc}(x_1, \ldots, x_{t})$. We make no commitment to its internal form. A recurrent network, a memory-augmented network, and a causal transformer are all admissible, and we use the encoder purely through the state $h_t$ it produces. The \emph{decoder}, or IRT readout, maps the state and a per-item embedding $e_i$ into interpretable coordinates and then into a category distribution. It exposes a student ability $\theta_t$, per-item parameters $\xi_i$, and a response model $R$ that turns them into logits $\eta = R(\xi_i; \theta_t)$. Different measurement models occupy the decoder slot by changing $R$ and $\xi_i$ without touching the encoder, and the encoder changes without touching the decoder. Training uses the prediction loss alone. No term supervises $\theta$, $\alpha$, or $\beta$ directly, and item parameters are read out of the trained weights after the fact.

The interface carries one design decision that this paper makes central, namely which inputs each readout is allowed to see. Ability characterizes the student and not the item being presented, so the natural choice is for $\theta_t$ to depend on the state alone, $\theta_t = \phi(h_t)$, and not on the item embedding $e_i$. Item parameters characterize the item and not the student, so they are functions of the item embedding. As Section~\ref{sec:method} shows, this apparently cosmetic wiring choice is exactly what decides whether the ability-difficulty split is identified, and widening or sharing the embedding that feeds a readout is exactly what sets the recovery rate of that readout. The two slots and their input wiring are therefore the levers we manipulate.

\subsection{Ordinal Encoding}
\label{sec:ordinal_encoding}
Because responses are ordered, the encoding of a response should preserve category distance, so that a score of $2$ is represented as more similar to $3$ than to $0$. We use a triangular, linear-decay kernel \citep{vargas_soft_2023} that spreads a response across neighboring categories,
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:linear_embed}
    s_t^{(k)} = \max\!\left(0, \; 1 - \frac{|k - r_t|}{K-1}\right), \qquad 0 \le k \le K-1,
\end{equation}
so that $\mathbf{s}_t = [s_t^{(0)}; \ldots; s_t^{(K-1)}]$. With $K=4$ and $r_t = 2$ this gives $\mathbf{s}_t = [0.33, 0.67, 1.0, 0.67]$. At $K=2$ the kernel reduces to a binary indicator, recovering the standard interaction encoding of binary KT \citep{zhang_dynamic_2017}. Item identity is fused with $\mathbf{s}_t$ through a learned item embedding, and the fused vector is what the encoder consumes. This encoding is orthogonal to the recovery questions we study, and we hold it fixed throughout.

\subsection{The Memory-Augmented Precursor}
\label{sec:precursor}
The framework grew out of a memory-augmented instance that pairs a Dynamic Key-Value Memory Network (DKVMN) encoder \citep{zhang_dynamic_2017} with a Generalized Partial Credit Model (GPCM) decoder \citep{van_der_linden_generalized_1997}, which we refer to as MA-GPCM and use as the precursor. Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} shows this instance. The DKVMN maintains a static key memory that represents latent skill areas and a dynamic value memory that tracks the student's evolving mastery, and item-determined attention weights read the value memory into a summary. The GPCM decoder turns that summary, together with the item key, into the ability and item parameters and a distribution over ordered categories.

The precursor already exhibits, as an architectural choice, the two input-wiring decisions this paper formalizes. Ability reads a separated summary that sees the memory read alone and not the item key, and step thresholds are a function of the item key alone. As we show next, the first is one instance of the ability-item decoupling that governs identifiability, and the split between the ability input and the item key is one instance of the width decoupling that governs recovery rate. \section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
We situate the work in three rings, ordered from the nearest scientific neighbors outward, and then state the anti-variational positioning that point two of the message rests on.

\subsection{Interpretable Knowledge Tracing and Neural IRT}
\label{sec:related_kt}
Deep knowledge tracing learns high-dimensional latent states from interaction sequences \citep{piech_deep_2015}, and the DKVMN introduced a structured memory that separates static concept representations from evolving mastery \citep{zhang_dynamic_2017}. A line of work then couples these encoders to IRT-style readouts for interpretability. Deep-IRT attaches an ability-and-difficulty readout to a DKVMN \citep{yeung_deep-irt_2019}, and \citet{converse_irt_kt_2021} incorporate IRT parameters into KT training more generally.\footnote{Cite-and-distinguish target [VERIFY]. We rely on the AIED 2021 version and confirm its recovery claims against its own shuffled-response analysis before submission.} These models expose parameters that a practitioner can inspect, and they argue interpretability from the presence of the parameters and from face validity, checking that estimated abilities and difficulties look reasonable. Our question is upstream of that argument. We ask whether the exposed parameters recover their generating values at all, and we find that the answer depends on which parameter, on the training budget, and on how the readout is wired. Recent independent-network variants such as \citet{tsutsumi_deep_2024} separate the student and item pathways for stability, which is consistent with the decoupling lever we analyze, though they do not connect the separation to a recovery-rate or identifiability mechanism.

\subsection{Discrimination-Recovery Failures}
\label{sec:related_disc}
Reports that item discrimination is the hardest parameter to recover recur across the neural and the psychometric literature, usually diagnosed as a nuisance rather than as a mechanism. \citet{converse_irt_kt_2021} observe degraded discrimination and connect it to data ordering. A beta-distributed IRT variant reports an unexplained shrinkage of the recovered discrimination magnitude in its recovery figure.\footnote{beta4-IRT [VERIFY]; confirm authors and the figure reference.} We read these as the same symptom, and we supply the mechanism. Discrimination sits on the low-information direction of the readout, so it relaxes slowly under gradient flow and is under-served at any finite budget, and the magnitude shrinkage others report is the expected signature of a slow, under-recovered coordinate rather than a separate defect.

\subsection{Option and Choice Modeling}
\label{sec:related_option}
The nominal response model gives each response category its own slope and intercept \citep{thissen_taxonomy_1986}, and it appears in KT as a diagnostic tool for tracing which distractor a student selects \citep{ghosh_option_2021}. We do not propose it as a better tracer. We repurpose it as a control, because its category slope loads on ability like discrimination but, unlike GPCM discrimination, is not low in information. That single dissociation is what lets us separate a representation effect from an information effect, as Section~\ref{sec:nrm} makes precise.

\subsection{The Anti-Variational Positioning}
\label{sec:related_var}
A distinct family recovers IRT parameters through variational inference. Variational IRT fits an amortized posterior over abilities and item parameters and reports accurate recovery \citep{wu_variational_2020}, and temporal variational IRT extends this to sequences and, notably, reports discrimination recovered best.\footnote{VTIRT [VERIFY]; confirm the venue and the discrimination-recovery claim.} A further variational neural IRT reports the same discrimination deficit we study and attributes it to the variational gap.\footnote{Ma et al. 2024 [VERIFY], arXiv:2310.12010; confirm authors, title, and venue.} We make two points about this family. First, those results are well-specified generative inference with an explicit posterior, not the plain prediction loss that KT actually trains on, so they do not speak to whether a standard KT tracer recovers its readouts. Second, where the variational literature attributes the discrimination deficit to the variational objective, our analysis locates it in the information geometry of the readout, which predicts the deficit even with no variational objective present. Our contribution is precisely that plain prediction loss recovers the parameters, and that the deficit that remains is a Fisher-geometry property rather than an artifact of a particular objective. For the downstream use of a recovered discrimination in adaptive testing we draw on the computerized-adaptive-testing literature \citep{gmocat_2023, autoirt_2024}, which we return to in Section~\ref{sec:downstream}.

\section{Method and Theory}
\label{sec:method}
This section formalizes recovery at the level of the decoder and the readout, so that the statements are encoder-agnostic. The home is the representation learning inside the KT encoder, and IRT supplies the readable coordinates whose recovery we track. We first fix the decoder math and the honest form of the per-parameter gradients, then state the rate result and its asymptotic behavior, then the identifiability result, and finally the nominal-model control. Each claim is labeled \textsc{finite} or \textsc{structural}.

\subsection{Decoder Math and Honest Gradients}
\label{sec:decoder_math}
The causal state is $h_t = \mathrm{enc}(x_1, \ldots, x_{t})$. The readout exposes per-item coordinates $\xi_i$ and an ability $\theta_t = \phi(h_t)$, optionally also conditioned on $e_i$. The response is categorical with logits $\eta = R(\xi_i; \theta_t)$, the loss is the expected cross-entropy $L = \mathbb{E}[\mathrm{CE}]$, the vector residual is $r_k = p_k - y_k$ with $p$ the predicted probabilities and $y$ the one-hot response, and the per-observation covariance is $\Sigma = \mathrm{diag}(p) - p\,p^\top$. Two decoders instantiate $R$. The GPCM sets a category logit through a cumulative sum,
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:gpcm_prob}
    p_{k} = \frac{\exp\!\left(\sum_{m=0}^{k-1} \alpha_i(\theta_t - \beta_{i,m})\right)}
                 {\sum_{c=0}^{K-1} \exp\!\left(\sum_{m=0}^{c-1} \alpha_i(\theta_t - \beta_{i,m})\right)},
\end{equation}
with discrimination $\alpha_i$ and step thresholds $\boldsymbol{\beta}_i = (\beta_{i,0}, \ldots, \beta_{i,K-2})$. The NRM gives each category its own slope and intercept,
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:nrm_prob}
    p_{k} = \mathrm{softmax}_k\!\left(a_{i,k}\,\theta_t + c_{i,k}\right),
\end{equation}
with slopes $a_{i,k}$ and intercepts $c_{i,k}$. The GPCM is a constrained NRM with $a_{i,k} = \alpha_i k$ \citep{thissen_taxonomy_1986}, a fact we use in Section~\ref{sec:nrm}.

We write the gradients in their honest form rather than in a compressed scalar shorthand. The clean scalar residual $r = p - y$ and the chain rule
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:binary_grads}
    \partial_\theta L = r\,\alpha, \qquad \partial_\beta L = -\,r\,\alpha, \qquad \partial_\alpha L = r\,(\theta - \beta)
\end{equation}
hold for the binary, two-parameter-logistic case, where the sigmoid-with-cross-entropy pairing makes $r = p - y$ the gradient of the loss with respect to the logit. For the general GPCM the corresponding statements use the vector residual $r_k = p_k - y_k$. The ability gradient is $\alpha_i$ times the expected-score residual, the difference between the model's expected category and the observed one, and the threshold gradient is a cumulative residual. We carry the binary setup together with the vector-residual generalization into the analysis, and we do not present a single scalar $r$ as the literal GPCM gradient. The structural facts the argument uses survive the generalization, that ability and difficulty enter the logit scaled by discrimination, that discrimination carries the separation leverage $\theta - \beta$, and that difficulty is the sign-flip of ability, $\partial_\beta L = -\partial_\theta L$. For the NRM the chain rule through the category logit, with $\partial_{a_k}\eta_k = \theta$ and $\partial_{c_k}\eta_k = 1$, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:nrm_grads}
    \partial_{a_k} L = r_k\,\theta, \qquad \partial_{c_k} L = r_k .
\end{equation}

\subsection{Recovery Rate and Its Asymptotics}
\label{sec:rate}
Fix the affine gauge (Section~\ref{sec:identif}) and work near a well-specified, zero-residual optimum, where the Gauss-Newton curvature equals the Fisher information $F$. Small deviations of a readout coordinate from the optimum then relax under gradient flow as
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:grad_flow}
    \dot{\delta} = -F\,\delta, \qquad \delta_c(t) = \delta_c(0)\,e^{-\lambda_c t},
\end{equation}
so each eigendirection decays at a rate set by its curvature, and a coordinate recovers in time of order $1/I(\xi)$. For a generic readout coordinate the per-coordinate information is the quadratic form
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:fisher_generic}
    I(\xi) = \left(\frac{\partial \eta}{\partial \xi}\right)^{\!\top} \Sigma \left(\frac{\partial \eta}{\partial \xi}\right).
\end{equation}

\begin{claim}[Rate, \textsc{finite}]\label{claim:rate}
In the binary case, the multiplier coordinates carry $I(\theta) = I(\beta) = \alpha^2 w$ with $w = p(1-p)$, while the discrimination coordinate carries $I(\alpha) = \mathbb{E}[\,w\,(\theta - \beta)^2\,]$. The discrimination information is suppressed exactly where the response mass concentrates, near $\theta \approx \beta$, so discrimination is the slow direction and recovers last. The ordering is read from the curvature alone, with no appeal to a link function or a conditioning number.
\end{claim}

The rate penalty is not permanent. Let the budget be a pair $(T, N)$ of gradient steps and observations, and let $\kappa = \lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min} \approx I(\theta)/I(\alpha)$ be the readout conditioning number.

\begin{claim}[Statistical decay, \textsc{finite}]\label{claim:decay}
On the optimization axis, with $N$ effectively infinite and a stability-bounded shared step size, the slow-mode residual after $T$ steps contracts as $\exp(-T/\kappa)$, so the recovery gap between discrimination and ability shrinks geometrically in steps. On the statistical axis, with $T$ effectively infinite, a free per-item table reaches the unbiased endpoint, and the residual reliability gap between $\alpha$ and $\theta$ is of order $\kappa/N$, plus an errors-in-variables bias of order $1/N$ on $\alpha$ from the noisy amortized ability estimate. Jointly the gap obeys $G(T, N) \lesssim \exp(-T/\kappa) + \mathcal{O}(\kappa/N)$, and both terms vanish as $\min(T, N)$ grows.
\end{claim}

Claim~\ref{claim:decay} licenses the honest headline of the paper. The discrimination penalty is real at any finite budget and it closes asymptotically, so the contribution lives in the realistic finite regime rather than in a claim of asymptotic impossibility. This is the classical statement that the flat direction of a well-specified model is learned slowly and estimated noisily \citep{amari_natural_1998, saxe_exact_2014}, translated to a KT readout trained by prediction.

\subsection{Coupling and Identifiability}
\label{sec:identif}
The GPCM logit $\alpha_i(\theta_t - \beta_i)$ carries a global affine gauge, the joint map $(\theta, \beta) \mapsto (s\theta + c, \; s\beta + c)$ with $\alpha \mapsto \alpha/s$, which has two parameters and is fixed by one normalization of location and one of scale. Whether more of the gauge is free depends on which inputs the ability readout sees.

\begin{claim}[Coupling, \textsc{structural}]\label{claim:coupling}
Let the ability readout be decoupled, $\theta_t = \phi(h_t)$, excluding the current item $i$. Then a single $\theta_t$ faces every item at step $t$, cannot re-center itself per item, and $\beta_i$ is identified up to the global gauge. Let it instead be coupled, $\theta_{t,i} = \phi(h_t, e_i)$. Then for each item the shift $(\theta_{t,i}, \beta_i) \mapsto (\theta_{t,i} + \delta_i, \; \beta_i + \delta_i)$ is a likelihood symmetry, and because $\phi$ already depends on $e_i$ it can absorb $\delta_i$. The gauge orbit enlarges from $2$ to $2 + J$ dimensions, one location shift per item, and those $J$ directions are exact flat directions of the population loss, so no estimator at any $N$ identifies $\beta_i$ within its orbit. The general condition is a measurability statement, that $\theta_t$ must be measurable with respect to a sigma-field that excludes $e_i$.
\end{claim}

This is the correct reading of the threshold collapse the precursor exhibits (Section~\ref{sec:swappable}). It is an identifiability failure, not a rate effect, and it persists at every budget. Regularization or finite width softens the exact flatness to near-flatness, but the property that it does not vanish with more data survives. The claim is stated at the readout level, and it is enforced by different architectural means in different encoders. The DKVMN separated pathway reads ability from the memory alone, and a recurrent or transformer encoder can enforce the same measurability by feeding ability a narrow item-value embedding while a separate wide item key feeds the item readouts. Neither trick is claimed to be encoder-generic; the math is the general statement, and the architectures are instances.

\subsection{Two Decouplings, Kept Distinct}
\label{sec:two_decouplings}
The word decoupling covers two mechanisms that sit on different axes, and the paper keeps them apart by construction.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Ability-item decoupling governs identifiability.} Removing $e_i$ from the ability readout fixes the location gauge of Claim~\ref{claim:coupling}. This is structural and persists at all budgets.
    \item \textbf{Width decoupling governs rate.} Giving a readout its own embedding width, rather than sharing one narrow width across ability and the item readouts, relieves the capacity trade-off that sets the conditioning number and hence the recovery rate. This is finite and vanishes asymptotically.
\end{itemize}
The nominal-model control of Section~\ref{sec:nrm} adjudicates the width mechanism only. It says nothing about the ability-item mechanism, and we do not let evidence for one stand in for the other. The identifiability mechanism is tested by manipulating the ability readout's inputs directly.

\subsection{The Nominal-Model Control}
\label{sec:nrm}
The NRM slope $a_k$ is the genuine discrimination analogue, because the GPCM is the constrained NRM with $a_k = \alpha k$ \citep{thissen_taxonomy_1986}. Its leverage on the logit is $\theta$ itself, centered at $\theta = 0$, which is away from the response mode, so its information is not suppressed the way GPCM discrimination is by the $(\theta - \beta)^2$ term that centers on the mode.

\begin{claim}[Nominal-model control, consistency]\label{claim:nrm}
The NRM slope and intercept carry near-equal information, with a measured ratio $I(a_k)/I(c_k) \approx 0.90$ across seeds, in contrast to the fivefold-to-tenfold discrimination-versus-difficulty gap of the GPCM. The NRM therefore separates a representation effect, in which width decoupling fires regardless of information, from an information effect, in which a state-conditioned head helps only a low-information coordinate. We report $0.90$ as measured; the intuition that the ratio is near one, from $\mathbb{E}[\theta^2] = 1$, is only heuristic because $p(1-p)$ co-varies with $\theta$.
\end{claim}

\subsection{Assumptions}
\label{sec:assumptions}
The leverage-sets-rate statement holds under a stated set of conditions, which we make explicit because the honest scope of the result depends on them. The analysis is local, near a gauge-fixed, identifiable, well-specified optimum with small residual. It assumes an encoder timescale separation, that the amortized ability settles fast relative to readout relaxation, or that ability is held at its true value, which is the oracle-clamp control we name in Section~\ref{sec:experiments_overview}. It assumes rank adequacy, that the shared code has rank at least $K$, since otherwise an expressivity wall rather than a rate controls recovery. The ordering is an eigenmode statement, and it holds for discrimination above a crossover value $a_\star \approx 1$; we return to the inversion below $a_\star$ in Section~\ref{sec:boundaries}. The raw conditioning-number penalty assumes a single global step size, since an adaptive optimizer preconditions parameters and compresses the penalty. Finally it assumes a softmax-cross-entropy-family loss, plain, class-weighted, or ordinal-weighted, where a detached positive per-sample weight enters as a scale and preserves the structure, and it breaks for earth-mover, margin, or regression-on-expected-category losses.

\subsection{Recovery Follows Fisher Information}
\label{sec:three_speeds}
The controlled prediction-loss study reads the recovery order directly off the training trajectory. Ability and difficulty recover fast, reaching rank recovery near $0.97$ and $0.99$ at the operating point, while discrimination recovers slowly and only later, in a band from about $0.70$ at a narrow embedding to about $0.92$ at a wide one. This is precisely the ordering that the curvatures of Claim~\ref{claim:rate} predict, the two high-information multiplier coordinates fast and the low-information separation coordinate slow, and it is read from the recovery dynamics with no appeal to a conditioning number. The finding is the empirical counterpart of the rate claim, and it holds at the realistic operating point rather than only at convergence. \emph{Strengthening.} A budget sweep over training steps and sample size, fitting the gap to the $\exp(-T/\kappa) + \mathcal{O}(\kappa/N)$ form of Claim~\ref{claim:decay} and adding an oracle-clamp arm that fixes ability at its true value, is the planned experiment E-budget that separates the training-time rate from the finite-sample variance and earns the finite-data framing against a train-longer objection.

\subsection{Static Versus Dynamic Decoupling}
\label{sec:static_dynamic}
Within the decoupled family there is a second choice, whether the discrimination readout is static or is conditioned on the learner state, and the honest comparison is mixed. On synthetic recovery, where the generating parameters are known, recovery is high and nearly flat across configurations, near $0.93$, so recovery cannot separate them. Split-half reliability, the agreement of a recovered parameter across two random halves of the learners, is the harder lens and does separate them. Reliability is lower and configuration-dependent, from about $0.75$ to about $0.84$, and the dynamic head is at least as reliable as the static head in every cell. The dynamic head thus helps, or at worst does not hurt, on the low-information discrimination channel, which is the Fisher lever of Section~\ref{sec:two_decouplings} acting where it should. The picture reverses on a parameter that is not low in information, as the next subsection shows. \emph{Strengthening.} A dedicated static-versus-dynamic comparison at eight or more seeds on synthetic recovery and on real reliability, with the nominal-model control run alongside, is the planned experiment E-levers that reports the mixed verdict with paired tests rather than from summary bands.

\subsection{Real Data}
\label{sec:real}
Real educational data carry no ground truth, so recovery against truth is unavailable and split-half reliability is the working lens, the real-data echo of recovery. We report two legs, the recovery geometry on proxy-ordinal tutoring data and the reliability behavior on large option-tracing data, and we state the honest verdict.

\subsubsection{Recovery Geometry on Proxy-Ordinal Tutoring Data}
\label{sec:assistments}
On the ASSISTments tutoring datasets, four ordered categories are derived from correctness together with attempt count and hint usage, so the labels are proxy-ordinal rather than rubric-scored, and the analysis reads model behavior rather than true item characteristics. Prediction quality is comparable across the GPCM-based models, within run-to-run variance of a softmax decoder on the ordinal metrics. The instructive contrast is in the recovered item geometry. The coupled shared-summary variant produces tightly clustered, sharply separated threshold modes, which looks like sharper measurement but is in fact a low-rank projection. Its ability estimate has absorbed the item-specific component of the proxy categories, which are themselves item-conditioned through hints and attempts, so residual item variation has been routed out of the thresholds and onto ability, collapsing the recovered item space toward a single difficulty axis. The separated variant refuses that absorption. Its thresholds spread per item, with partial overlap of adjacent modes, which recovers the higher-dimensional transition structure the proxy actually carries, at the cost of a small ordinal-metric decrement. Figure~\ref{fig:assist} shows the contrast. This is the same identifiability mechanism as the synthetic threshold collapse, now visible without ground truth, and it is why the separated readout is the one to deploy when the item parameters are meant to be read. We expect the decrement to narrow on rubric-anchored data, where the score levels reflect proficiency rather than item-specific behavior.

\subsection{Downstream: Adaptive Item Selection}
\label{sec:downstream}
The recovery findings change a concrete downstream decision, which is how an adaptive test selects the next item. Adaptive testing selects items to maximize information at the running ability estimate, and item information peaks where ability meets difficulty and scales with discrimination, so a selection rule is a joint function of the recovered discrimination and difficulty in which discrimination is the degraded channel. The planned evaluation selects items by maximum information at the running ability estimate, using each configuration's recovered parameters against a per-item oracle, and scores the configurations by ability error at a fixed test length, against an oracle ceiling and a random-selection floor. This is the experiment E8, and it is described here as planned. We report no adaptive-testing numbers, because the point of the rigorous rebuild is to measure this decision under a clean protocol rather than to assert it. The second genuinely new experiment, the classical cross-calibration of Section~\ref{sec:ednet}, is likewise planned and carries no numbers.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Two Levers and a Practice Recommendation}
\label{sec:two_levers}
The study resolves into two levers with different scopes, and confusing them is the mistake to avoid. The representation lever, a decoupled embedding with a narrow ability input and a separate wide item key, governs the shared-capacity trade-off and, applied to the ability readout, restores the location split. It fires whenever a readout shares a representation with ability, whether or not that readout is low in information, and it is the robust one. The information lever, a state-conditioned discrimination head, helps a low-information parameter and can hurt one that is not. The nominal-model control is what keeps them apart, because on a slope that is not low in information the representation lever still fires while the information lever reverses.

For practice this yields two recommendations sized to the evidence. First, give a readout its own embedding when it shares a representation with ability, because any slope on ability pays the shared-width trade-off and a separate wide key clears it whether or not its information is low, and because separating the ability read from the item is what makes the item parameters identifiable in the first place. Second, treat a dynamic discrimination head as a targeted fix and not a default, because it revives the low-information discrimination channel but degrades a slope whose information is not low, and on the ability channel of real option-tracing data a static decoupled configuration is the reliability winner. The practical default is therefore a static decoupled configuration, with the dynamic head reserved for the discrimination channel where its information is low.

\subsection{Boundaries and Threats}
\label{sec:boundaries}
We keep the honest caveats on the record rather than hiding them, because several are load-bearing for how far the claims reach.

The rate law is per eigenmode, not per parameter. The Fisher matrix has an off-diagonal coupling between discrimination and difficulty, so the slow eigenvector is discrimination-dominated only when the mean discrimination exceeds the crossover $a_\star \approx 1$, and below that value the ordering inverts and difficulty becomes the slower coordinate. We state the inversion and we plan to locate the crossover empirically.

The equality of Gauss-Newton curvature and Fisher information holds only at a zero-residual, well-specified optimum. On misspecified real data the curvature and the information differ, and a residual gap need not vanish, so the statement that the rate penalty vanishes asymptotically is a within-model statement. On real data the evidence is reliability, and with the planned cross-calibration also calibration, rather than recovery against truth.

The contraction shape $\exp(-T/\kappa)$ is a claim about form and sign, not a fitted coefficient. Over a category-count sweep the conditioning number and the category count move together, so the two cannot be told apart from a category sweep alone, and isolating the conditioning number needs a separate knob that varies the ability information at fixed category count, which we plan through an item-difficulty-spread sweep.

The central open gap is the co-learned ability. The ability estimate is amortized and learned jointly with the readouts, so its effective rate is the Fisher rate attenuated by pooling variance, and the load-bearing object is the curvature restricted to the shared code, with the slow direction living in code space rather than on the bare discrimination axis. The oracle-clamp control, which fixes ability at its true value and recovers items only, is the check that bridges this gap, and it is part of the planned rebuild.

Finally, the theory is classical in parts, drawing on natural-gradient learning and the spectral dynamics of learning \citep{amari_natural_1998, saxe_exact_2014}, and we sell it as a translation to knowledge tracing rather than as new psychometrics. The swappability claim is demonstrated on the swaps we ran and is being extended by the planned swap matrix, and no single architecture's trick is claimed to be encoder-generic.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Knowledge tracing models attach an item-response-theory readout so that a prediction can be read as an ability, a discrimination, and a difficulty, and the field has largely taken the readouts at face value. We treated their recovery as the object of study. Built as two swappable slots and trained by plain prediction loss with no variational machinery, the framework recovers its readouts on synthetic ground truth, but unevenly and in a stable order in which discrimination returns last. A local analysis explains the order as a finite-budget rate effect, in which the low-information direction relaxes slowly and closes only asymptotically, alongside a structural identifiability effect, in which coupling the ability readout to the item breaks the location split at every budget. Configuration is the lever. A decoupled embedding escapes the shared-capacity trade-off and restores the split, a state-conditioned head helps only a low-information parameter, and the nominal response model separates the two because its slope loads on ability but is not low in information. On real data the honest verdict is a static decoupled configuration, with the dynamic head a targeted fix and the decoupling benefit contingent on coverage. The design pattern that follows is to give a readout its own embedding when it shares a representation with ability, and to reserve state-conditioning for the channel whose information is low. The rigorous rebuild under one protocol, and the downstream adaptive-testing evaluation, are the steps that will turn this directional evidence into a measured result.

\section{GPCM and NRM Gradients and Fisher Information}
\label{app:gradients}
For the binary two-parameter-logistic case, with logit $z = \alpha(\theta - \beta)$, predicted probability $p = \sigma(z)$, and residual $r = p - y$, the loss gradients are those of Equation~\eqref{eqn:binary_grads}, $\partial_\theta L = r\alpha$, $\partial_\beta L = -r\alpha$, and $\partial_\alpha L = r(\theta - \beta)$. The per-coordinate Fisher information follows from Equation~\eqref{eqn:fisher_generic} with $\Sigma = w = p(1-p)$, giving $I(\theta) = I(\beta) = \alpha^2 w$ and $I(\alpha) = \mathbb{E}[w(\theta - \beta)^2]$. For the general GPCM the residual is the vector $r_k = p_k - y_k$, the ability gradient is $\alpha_i$ times the expected-score residual, and the threshold gradients are cumulative residuals, with the covariance $\Sigma = \mathrm{diag}(p) - pp^\top$ entering the information as the quadratic form of Equation~\eqref{eqn:fisher_generic}. For the NRM the gradients are those of Equation~\eqref{eqn:nrm_grads}, $\partial_{a_k} L = r_k \theta$ and $\partial_{c_k} L = r_k$, and the measured information ratio is $I(a_k)/I(c_k) \approx 0.90$ across seeds, reported as measured because $p(1-p)$ co-varies with $\theta$.

\section{Gauge Handling}
\label{app:gauge}
The GPCM logit carries the affine gauge $(\theta, \beta) \mapsto (s\theta + c, s\beta + c)$ with $\alpha \mapsto \alpha/s$, two parameters fixed by one location and one scale normalization. Rank metrics need only sign alignment and are gauge-free. Level metrics fix the two-parameter affine gauge by a single global linear map from recovered to true values, never a per-item map, because a per-item map would absorb exactly the per-item location shifts that the identifiability failure of Claim~\ref{claim:coupling} produces. Under a coupled ability readout a global affine map cannot rescue the per-item flat directions, so degraded threshold rank recovery is itself the identifiability signature rather than a scaling artifact.

\section{Precursor Synthetic Details}
\label{app:magpcm}
The precursor's synthetic protocol simulates static ability, discrete ability shifts, and continuous drift from a known GPCM, with abilities standard normal, discrimination log-normal with moderate spread, and ordered thresholds per item. The recovery order and the threshold collapse of Section~\ref{sec:swappable} hold across all three regimes and across item banks from two hundred to two thousand items, and they persist under right-skewed response distributions. At two categories the decoder reduces to a two-parameter-logistic head and the model recovers standard binary knowledge tracing, so the framework subsumes binary KT as a special case. A category-count sweep over three to six categories shows the recovery order unchanged, with discrimination the hardest parameter at every resolution and the threshold split between the coupled and separated variants widening as the resolution grows. The training objective is a weighted ordinal loss that penalizes far misclassifications more than near ones, a detail orthogonal to the recovery questions and held fixed throughout.

\section{Reproducibility}
\label{app:repro}
The controlled study fixes $N=800$ learners, $Q=60$ items, and $K=4$ categories on a recurrent encoder, trained by prediction loss and read at a $150$-epoch operating point with at least eight seeds and $95\%$ confidence intervals. The planned rebuild separates data seeds from initialization seeds, uses paired comparisons with bootstrap confidence intervals, records per-cell configuration hashes and an environment manifest, and reports every experiment as one point in a single protocol. Item parameters are recovered post hoc from frozen weights, item-level estimates are averaged across encounters before scoring, and reliability is odd-even split-half with a Spearman-Brown correction, per parameter.
