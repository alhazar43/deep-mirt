## v0 — anchoring drift, static (commit 6813c3b)
- **Q.** Can a deep ability scale absorb new items without recalibrating?
- **Setup.** Small LSTM + GPCM, synthetic, B=100 / E=30 / N=2000 / K=4, static theta. Anchoring = freeze model and old item embeddings, fit only new-item embeddings against pinned theta.
- **Result.** New-item recovery within threshold (anchor a_corr .951 / b .987 vs baseline .964 / .991); cost 31x fewer params, 37x faster.
- **Verdict.** Calibration half supported. The original tracking metric was bogus (never fed E through the encoder).
- **Next.** Fix the tracking test → v0.1.

## v0.1 — real tracking test + distribution shift (commit 6813c3b)
- **Q.** Does the frozen encoder still track a learner who then answers the new items?
- **Setup.** Interleaved B+E sequences through the frozen extended encoder; two venues (same-dist E, shifted +1.0).
- **Result.** Tracking PASSES. Same-dist theta_BplusE vs truth .979 = B-only .979 (no degradation); shift +1.0 .976. Soft spot: under shift, new-item threshold calibration degrades more than baseline (b_corr .979 vs .986).
- **Verdict.** Central static claim survives. The embedding double-duty risk did not bite.

## Pilot — SLAM respondent-agnostic transfer (not committed)
- **Q.** Does LLM item difficulty (models genuinely attempting) predict human item difficulty (the "one slot" premise)?
- **Setup.** 200 stratified SLAM en_es text items (reverse_translate / reverse_tap), 4 Qwen3.5 models (2B–7B) translate ES→EN via Ollama, graded by token overlap, vs human token-error difficulty.
- **Result.** No ceiling (models 9.6–11.7% error, real variance). Transfer WEAK: Spearman 0.20, Pearson 0.31. On the difficulty-driven subset (hd>0.1), Pearson 0.39.
- **Diagnosis.** Construct mismatch, not ceiling or null. SLAM human difficulty reflects Duolingo's strict exact-match grading (phrasing, word order), not translation difficulty. Many items humans "fail" on phrasing while the models translate perfectly. SLAM human difficulty is a contaminated proxy for item difficulty.
- **Verdict.** Transfer is real but weak and confounded by the grading construct. Not a clean pass, not a refutation (the "shared machinery, separate scales" fallback holds). SLAM is a poor testbed for this construct.
- **Tightening (per-format).** Hypothesis confirmed. reverse_tap (tap from given tiles, no phrasing variation) Spearman 0.36 / Pearson 0.49 (n=47); reverse_translate 0.28 / 0.37 (n=153); overall 0.20 / 0.31. 9/200 (4.5%) artifact-suspect items (LLM aces, human fails on grading). The weak overall number was the free-typing grading confound, not absent transfer.
- **Revised verdict.** Transfer is real and moderate on the clean format. The "one slot" premise holds on clean items. Eedi not needed now; the clean signal is inside SLAM on reverse_tap.
- **Decision.** Scale up on reverse_tap (~500 items, widest model spread) to firm the estimate. Gate Spearman >= ~0.35 robustly = agnosticism keystone supported on clean real data, then advance the backlog.
- **Scale-up: infra chaos, but it completed.** The scale-up agent went zombie, spawning competing Ollama runs + watchers (one even nohup'd to survive kills); 5+ runaway python accumulated and could not be killed from this shell. Lesson: run long external-process jobs via main-loop background bash only, one at a time, never delegated to a babysitting subagent.
- **Scale-up RESULT (n=486, completed exit 0).** Spearman **0.341**, Pearson **0.424**, Kendall 0.254 at n=486 (statistically robust). No ceiling (per-model error 12–16%), artifact rate 2.1%. Hypothesized limiter = model homogeneity (all 5 Qwen 2–7B, error spread 0.038, 50.8% zero-error items), so 0.34 framed as a lower bound. **Verdict: keystone supported at a moderate level** — item difficulty transfers between humans and small LLMs on clean items.
- **POOL DIVERSIFICATION (2026-06-13, deep_irt/bench/outputs/) — REFUTES the homogeneity hypothesis.** Reran the n=486 reverse_tap protocol with a DIVERSE pool spanning 4 families and 1B-3.8B (llama3.2:1b/3b, qwen3.5:2B, phi4-mini:3.8b, gemma4:e2b). The diversification WORKED at the variance level: model error spread 0.038 -> **0.188** (5x), zero-error items 50.8% -> 31.3%, LLM difficulty now spans 0-1. BUT the transfer is **UNCHANGED: Spearman 0.345** (vs 0.341), Pearson 0.413. So model-pool homogeneity was NOT the limiter — directly falsified by test. **The real limiter is SLAM's GRADING CONSTRUCT**: human difficulty reflects Duolingo exact-match grading (phrasing/word-order), a contaminated proxy for intrinsic item difficulty; a diverse pool can't fix a construct mismatch (9 artifact items: LLMs ace, humans fail on phrasing). REVISED VERDICT: the ~0.34 transfer is real, moderate, and now shown ROBUST to pool composition (stable across two very different pools) — STRONGER support for respondent-agnosticism, but it saturates at ~0.34 on SLAM and will not climb by adding models. To push higher needs a cleaner-construct testbed (MCQ difficulty, no exact-match artifact). EdNet TOEIC MCQ (already on disk) is the natural candidate — clean a/b/c/d grading. Process note: ran single-flight via main-loop background bash (lesson applied); Ollama allocated full 128K context (slow, CPU-spilled) — cap context on future runs. tap_scaled_transfer.py made model-list env-overridable (RQ3_MODELS). ## Bradley-Terry decoder-versatility demo (synthetic)
- **Q.** Do direct ordinal responses (GPCM, human-style) and pairwise comparisons (Bradley-Terry, judge-style) recover the same latent item scale?
- **Setup.** N=200 items, one latent scale; Mode A = GPCM ordinal responses from 2000 respondents; Mode B = 5000 pairwise comparisons. Two independent fits.
- **Result. PASS.** Cross-format agreement (GPCM difficulty vs BT strength): Spearman 0.92, Pearson 0.91. Each mode vs truth: 0.95–0.97.
- **Verdict.** The two response formats recover one coherent item dimension. Honest qualifier: shared synthetic ground truth means this proves the format-versatility *mechanism*, not respondent-agnosticism (that is SLAM's role, moderate 0.34). Together: strong synthetic mechanism + moderate real-data transfer.
- **Caveat that motivated the next experiment.** These were two INDEPENDENT fits, then compared. They agree only because both recover the same true scale that generated the data. The model is not doing the unifying; the shared ground truth is. The deck's cross-format slide rested on this, and it was a real hole (the model "did nothing").

## Joint-format transfer — the shared representation does the unifying (deep_irt/jointfmt/, synthetic)
- **Q.** Does ONE shared representation genuinely unify formats? Test: an item seen in only one format, read out on the other format's scale. Independent fits cannot do this by construction (an unseen item has no position in that format).
- **Setup.** 300 items on one latent scale, partitioned DIRECT-ONLY (100, GPCM data only) / PAIRWISE-ONLY (100, BT data only) / BOTH (100, the bridge). One shared item embedding → one shared `DifficultyExtractor` → scalar d_i that BOTH heads read (GPCM difficulty centroid AND BT strength are the same d_i). Joint train GPCM+BT. Baseline: same architecture but SEPARATE embedding tables and extractors per format (only the sharing differs).
- **Result. PASS, decisively.** Pairwise-only items placed on the ordinal scale: Spearman **0.989** (independent GPCM, never saw them: **−0.057**, noise). Direct-only items placed on the pairwise scale: **0.977** (independent BT: **0.161**, near-noise). Transfer gain +1.05 / +0.82. Sanity: each independent model recovers its OWN items fine (GPCM on direct-only 0.977, BT on pairwise-only 0.989), so the gap is the sharing, not broken architecture. Joint overall 0.976; BOTH items 0.99.
- **Verdict.** The shared scale genuinely unifies the formats. An item measured one way lands correctly when read the other way, purely because the BOTH-item bridge orients one shared embedding space. This is the model doing the work, and supersedes the independent-fit demo's weak claim. `fig_crossformat` regenerated to the honest joint-vs-independent two-panel contrast; deck Result 2 slide rewritten to claim this.

## RQ2 — item-agnosticism on REAL data, first real-data experiment (deep_irt/slam_extend/, 2026-06-13)
- **Q.** Does anchored extension (freeze encoder + base item embeddings, fit only new-item embeddings, theta pinned to base) reproduce a FULL RECALIBRATION for new items on real SLAM, cheaply, without disturbing the existing scale? No synthetic ground truth — reference is full-recal + its own seed-reliability ceiling.
- **Setup.** SLAM en_es, NAMED items only (4995, min_count=10; the 3 per-format catch-all buckets dropped to avoid train/test leakage), 70/30 BASE/NEW split, GPCM K=3, LSTM encoder hidden=64. Bridge = the new deep_irt/core/realdata.py (masking + mini-batch 256). Anchored ext vs full-recal x2 seeds; metric = Spearman(anchored, full-recal) as a fraction of the seed-to-seed ceiling. Random split + a difficulty-STRESS split (new = hardest 30%). 100 epochs/fit.
- **Result — PARTIAL (both splits consistent).** Random: difficulty ceiling 0.835, anchored difficulty 0.631 (**0.76x ceiling**), discrimination 0.340 (0.46x). Difficulty-stress: ceiling 0.854, anchored difficulty 0.636 (**0.75x ceiling**), discrimination 0.174 (0.25x). DIFFICULTY recovery is ROBUST to adversarial item selection (0.76x -> 0.75x, no degradation); DISCRIMINATION collapses (0.46x -> 0.25x) and is weak throughout. **Cost: extension 6.5-12.6s vs full-recal 866-1374s (~100x faster), 0.228x params.**
- **The deeper finding = the LOW CEILING.** Two full recalibrations of the SAME data (different seeds) agree only ~0.84 on difficulty, ~0.72 on discrimination, in BOTH splits. The freely-estimated neural scale is only moderately seed-reproducible on real data — empirically consistent with P0.1's weak-identifiability theory (Xi & Bloem-Reddy 2023; VIBO). Reframes anchoring from "cheap approximation of the right answer" to "a way to FIX a reference the free re-estimation does not reproduce anyway."
- **Verdict.** PARTIAL: item DIFFICULTY transfers to the real scale at ~3/4 of a noisy ceiling, robustly, ~100x cheaper; discrimination does not transfer reliably. Honest first real-data result, not a clean win.
- **CONVERGENCE CHECK RESOLVED IT (250 epochs, random-only, outputs_conv/) — verdict UPGRADES to PASS on difficulty.** 100ep -> 250ep: anchored difficulty 0.631 -> 0.676, fraction of ceiling 0.755 -> **0.845 (PASS, >= 0.80x bar)**. So part of the "partial" was undertraining; with convergence, anchored extension recovers new-item DIFFICULTY at 0.85x the full-recal reliability ceiling. The KEY twist: the ceiling did NOT rise with training, it drifted DOWN (0.835 -> 0.801) — more training makes the two full-recal seeds agree LESS, each settling into its own optimum. Direct empirical confirmation of weak identifiability (P0.1 / Xi & Bloem-Reddy): the freely re-estimated neural scale has no unique difficulty ordering. So anchoring is a STABILIZER for an inherently non-unique estimation, not a lossy shortcut. HONEST hedge: ceiling is a 2-seed estimate so the DROP is within noise; the robust claim is it sits ~0.80 and does not improve with training. Discrimination stays unrecoverable at 250ep too (frac 0.37). theta base-vs-recal 0.48 (the "churn" partly = legitimate info gain, do not overclaim).

- **WHY the initial discrimination null (the structural finding, survives the engine swap).** The premise — "ma-irt's GPCM recovers alpha at 0.93, so anchoring should too" — conflates JOINT-FIT alpha (read from per-step joint summaries the encoder builds WHILE training on the item) with ANCHORED COLD-START alpha (reconstructed for an item the frozen encoder never trained on, from a pinned per-learner state). ma-irt's alpha = `exp(disc_net([joint_summary_t, item_emb]))` is STATE-CONDITIONED PER STEP; anchoring pins ONE summary per learner, so it cannot reproduce the per-step variation that carries the discrimination signal. The decoder's quality is irrelevant once that signal is gone. The seed-reliability CEILING confirms it: discrimination ceiling is only ~0.65-0.70 in full-fit (vs ~0.79-0.85 difficulty), so SLAM discrimination is intrinsically noisy at the source. **The cap is the ANCHORING PROTOCOL (pinned per-learner state), not the decoder — the same structural reason the substrate fails, and it survives the engine upgrade.** Confound caught: at 2 epochs the smoke test showed an apparent 1.1x PASS — an undertraining artifact (untrained full-recal alpha is trivially matched); only the trained 80-epoch run reveals the true ~0.44x.
- **DISCRIMINATION NULL UNDER RE-EXAMINATION — temporal-aggregation confound (user-caught, code-confirmed 2026-06-13).** The discrimination measurement above is APPLES-TO-ORANGES and the "structural cap" conclusion is therefore PROVISIONAL. `recover_full` (the reference) reads per-step alpha at the REAL encounter state and averages over (learner, step) occurrences — i.e. alpha is temporal + student-averaged, refined over steps. But the anchored extraction conditions alpha on a SINGLE pinned final-base `joint_summary` per learner, `.expand`ed constant across all steps — the per-step refinement is discarded BEFORE the average. The 0.44x null is contaminated by this aggregation mismatch, NOT cleanly structural. RESOLVED -> see next bullet.

## P1.6 — dynamic encoder x joint decoder PACK (deep_irt/dynjoint/, synthetic, 2026-06-13)

Do dynamic ability tracking AND format-agnosticism hold TOGETHER in one encoder-driven pack? This is the literal "encoder-decoder KT+IRT pack" (user's architectural frame: IRT is a flavour, richer side-info both ends, intermediate KT outputs exposable).
- **Setup.** LSTM encoder (sequence -> time-varying theta_t) REPLACES jointfmt's static theta Parameter; shared item embedding -> shared DifficultyExtractor -> scalar d_i feeding BOTH a GPCM head (ordinal, uses theta_t + d_i) and a BT head (pairwise, person-free). PASS — both hold together.** Cross-format transfer survives the encoder: pairwise-only items land on the ordinal scale at Spearman **0.968** (vs independent noise -0.050; static-theta ref 0.990), direct-only on the pairwise scale at **0.861** (vs noise +0.192; static ref 0.980). - **Richer-pack interface delivered (user's frame).** `DynJointModel.get_kt_state(item_ids, responses) -> (h_n, c_n)` exposes the LSTM hidden+cell as a KT mastery readout (W@h_n -> per-skill mastery) — intermediate KT output made first-class. theta interface kept general for future multidim unpacking; item-embedding input structured to later accept content side-info.
- **Verdict.** The encoder-decoder KT+IRT pack EXISTS and is validated synthetically: dynamic tracking + format-agnostic shared scale + exposable KT state in one model. Closes G4.
- **REBALANCED (ml-system-architect a5114b93e9ec7e96c).** direct-only->BT improved **0.873 -> 0.954** (3-seed mean), pairwise-only flat (0.977), net-drift tracking IMPROVED 0.433 -> 0.502, at no cost. Joins RQ2 (anchoring > free re-estimation) and RQ5 (anchored readout > free encoder readout): a freely/over-optimized learned neural ability scale becomes unstable/non-transferable; a structural constraint (anchoring / fixed-param readout / early stop) preserves it. Theory: Xi & Bloem-Reddy (a deep latent scale needs structure to be identifiable). Next: apply pack to real mixed-format data (rides on RQ1).

## RQ5/RQ4 — separability of a LEARNED ability scale across instruments (deep_irt/ednet_sep/, real EdNet, 2026-06-13)
- **Q.** Is the learned neural ability scale separable/invariant across instruments (TOEIC parts 1-7)? The open gap P0.1 flagged (nobody audits invariance of a LEARNED scale). Human EdNet multi-instrument data; research-scientist (opus) designed+ran.
- **Design.** One joint model on all parts (shared scale); per-part ability via frozen encoder on part-restricted subsequences; split-half for per-part reliability. RQ5b variance decomposition, RQ5a leakage probe (with scalar control), RQ4 cross-part alignment (with overlap gradient).
- **Result — NOT cleanly separable, but a SPECIFIC, interesting failure.** RQ5a: the ABILITY AXIS is instrument-agnostic (naive leakage AUC 0.82 is ITEM-CONTENT from disjoint banks; scalar-ability control AUC = **0.51 = chance** -> the encoding does NOT carry instrument). RQ5b: separability index **0.16** (person-variance share); part MAIN effect ~0 but **person×part INTERACTION dominates ~0.84** (robust across grids) -> a learner's ability swings idiosyncratically part-to-part. RQ4: disattenuated cross-part consistency uniformly **~0.41** (vs 1.0 a separable scale predicts), **NO construct-distance gradient** (within-section 0.43 ≈ cross-section 0.39).
- **Read.** The encoder builds ONE instrument-agnostic ability axis (one scale holds at the encoding level), but a learner's POSITION on it is weakly cross-instrument-consistent.
